\section{Chapter Overview}
\label{sec:chapter_overview}
Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) has evolved rapidly over recent decades, driven by the search for natural, intuitive, and ubiquitous interfaces that transcend the constraints of physical input peripherals. Standard physical input devices, such as mechanical QWERTY keyboards and capacitive touchpads, remain the primary modalities for text entry and command execution in desktop and mobile computing. However, their physical form factor, susceptibility to mechanical wear, high manufacturing overhead, and complete lack of spatial layout adaptability restrict their utility in emerging computing paradigms, ubiquitous computing, cleanroom environments, and personalized accessibility contexts \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020, Lee2022Virtual}.

This research introduces a novel, customizable paper-based virtual keyboard system powered by monocular computer vision, fiducial planar tracking, and deep recurrent neural networks. By combining printable paper layouts containing fiducial marker anchors, monocular vision pose tracking, hand-length scale normalization (without temporal smoothing), dynamic homography transformation, and a custom PyTorch Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) finger touch classification model, the proposed architecture turns any flat physical surface into an interactive input interface using standard commodity RGB webcams.

\section{Problem Background}
\label{sec:problem_background}
The fundamental interaction paradigm between humans and computing machinery has relied predominantly on physical electromechanical keyboards for over half a century. While physical keyboards provide tactile feedback and high typing throughput for proficient typists, their static physical boundaries present significant operational limitations \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020}. Physical keyboards are inherently rigid; their key sizes, layout configurations, and spatial arrangements cannot be dynamically reconfigured to accommodate domain-specific command sets, non-standard language character sets, single-handed input requirements, or assistive configurations for individuals with motor impairments.

To overcome the rigid nature of physical hardware, research in visual computer vision interfaces has investigated projection-based and camera-based virtual keyboards \cite{Zhang2001Visual, Srivastava2012RealTime, Khare2019QWERTY, Ji2018Local}. Early virtual keyboard implementations relied heavily on optical projection units coupled with specialized infrared sensors or time-of-flight depth cameras \cite{Lee2019Virtual, Kudale2016RealTime}. While depth sensors provide explicit Z-axis distance measurements to infer surface contact, they require specialized, costly hardware that prevents widespread deployment on standard mobile devices or commodity PCs. Furthermore, classical single-camera RGB approaches attempted touch detection by analyzing optical shadows projected under user fingertips \cite{Thomas2013Camera, Posner2012Single}. However, shadow-based techniques suffer severe instability under variable ambient illumination, directional light changes, dynamic self-occlusion, and non-uniform background surfaces, resulting in high false-positive keystroke rates and unreliable operational stability \cite{Yue2014Blind}.

Recent advancements in deep learning and real-time hand joint estimation frameworks, such as Google MediaPipe Hand Landmarker, have enabled markerless 2D and 3D hand skeleton tracking directly from commodity RGB video feeds \cite{Zhang2020MediaPipe, Sreenath2021Monocular, GilMartin2023Hand, Ivacko2024Edge, Andriyanov2025Improving}. Skeletal keypoint localization operates robustly across skin pigmentation variations and lighting fluctuations by anchoring predictions to anatomical hand landmark structures rather than fragile skin-color thresholding \cite{SanchezBrizuela2023Lightweight}. However, despite high frame-rate keypoint localization, converting 3D/2D spatial hand joint landmarks into accurate physical surface touch events remains a major technical challenge \cite{Li2021CNN, Lee2022Virtual}. Variations in hand-to-camera distance, hand size differences, dynamic perspective tilt, and the lack of physical tactile feedback make simple thresholding of raw fingertip positions inadequate for detecting deliberate touch actions on flat planes \cite{Maman2023TypeNet, Yoo2026WordLevel}. 

Moreover, existing vision-based input systems often lack a seamless mechanism to map physical surface interactions to custom, user-designed key layouts. Without persistent visual anchors and planar perspective correction, small camera shifts or surface displacements invalidate coordinate mappings, making continuous typing input erratic \cite{Wang2016AprilTag2, Kallwies2020Determining, Shabalina2018ARTag, Kalaitzakis2021Fiducial, Pirchheim2011Homography, Zhang2022DynaKey}. Consequently, there is an urgent need for an integrated framework that unifies custom paper layout design, low-latency fiducial planar calibration, scale-normalized landmark feature extraction (without temporal smoothing), deep learning sequential touch detection, and dynamic homography coordinate transformation.

\section{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:problem_statement}
Despite extensive research into vision-based text entry systems, current monocular virtual keyboard solutions struggle with the dual challenge of robust touch detection on flat surfaces and flexible planar layout mapping without specialized hardware.

\subsection{General Problem}
\label{subsec:general_problem}
At a high level, traditional computing input hardware relies on fixed physical peripherals that impose substantial economic costs, susceptibility to liquid damage and mechanical wear, hygienic contamination risks in public or medical environments, and physical immutability \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020}. While visual virtual keyboards offer a software-driven touchless alternative, existing implementations predominantly rely on costly depth sensors, active laser projection devices, or specialized motion capture infrastructure \cite{Lee2019Virtual, Pilkov2024Markerless}. This hardware dependency undermines the accessibility, low-cost portability, and rapid deployability that virtual interfaces promise.

\subsection{Specific Problem}
\label{subsec:specific_problem}
Within the domain of computer vision and human-computer interaction, monocular RGB camera systems face fundamental technical shortcomings when attempting surface touch detection and spatial layout interaction:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Ambiguity in Z-Axis Contact Estimation:} Monocular RGB images lack explicit depth information. Existing vision-based approaches attempting shadow analysis or static position thresholding \cite{Thomas2013Camera, Srivastava2012RealTime} suffer high error rates due to illumination fluctuations, complex background textures, and variations in finger morphology.
    \item \textbf{Spatial Scale Variance and Kinematic Feature Extraction:} Real-time skeletal pose detectors output raw pixel or normalized screen coordinates that vary with hand-to-camera distance and user hand dimensions \cite{Zhang2020MediaPipe, Kumar2026Hybrid, Andriyanov2025Improving}. Without hand-length scale normalization, kinetic positional and derived velocity features vary drastically across users and operating distances \cite{Simao2016Unsupervised}.
    \item \textbf{Perspective Drift and Spatial Misalignment:} Physical surfaces captured by monocular cameras are subject to arbitrary perspective distortions and camera pose variation \cite{Wang2016AprilTag2, Oscadal2020Improved}. Static geometric layout templates fail whenever the camera or physical paper shifts, resulting in inaccurate coordinate transformations \cite{Kallwies2020Determining, Kalaitzakis2021Fiducial, Pirchheim2011Homography, Herdiansyah2024Implementation, GarridoJurado2026ArUco}.
    \item \textbf{Rigid non-configurable Layouts:} Conventional virtual keyboard implementations enforce static QWERTY configurations, lacking integration with user-customizable software tools capable of defining arbitrary key geometries, command triggers, and planar fiducial anchor targets \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Research Gap:} A significant research gap exists in developing a unified monocular computer vision pipeline that combines \emph{printable paper-based customization with fiducial AprilTag homography tracking} \cite{Wang2016AprilTag2, Kallwies2020Determining}, \emph{hand-length scale normalization (without temporal smoothing)} \cite{Zhang2020MediaPipe}, and a \emph{custom PyTorch LSTM deep temporal classifier} \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM, Gammulle2021TMMF} capable of evaluating sliding window feature sequences to accurately detect finger-to-surface touch events in real time without relying on depth sensors, shadow conditions, or temporal low-pass filters.

\section{Research Question}
\label{sec:research_question}
To address the identified research gap, this study is guided by the following primary research question:

\begin{quote}
\textit{How can a monocular RGB vision framework integrate dynamic fiducial homography tracking, hand-length scale normalization, and deep sequential touch classification to enable accurate, real-time, customizable paper-based virtual key interaction on arbitrary flat surfaces?}
\end{quote}

Sub-investigations associated with this central question include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item How can scale normalization be structured on MediaPipe hand joint trajectories to achieve distance- and hand-size-invariant features without relying on temporal low-pass filtering or smoothing?
    \item What sliding window duration and stride parameters enable a deep LSTM neural network to maximize finger touch classification accuracy while maintaining low temporal latency?
    \item How effectively does AprilTag planar homography transformation correct camera perspective distortion and physical paper movement during active typing interactions?
\end{itemize}

\section{Research Motivation}
\label{sec:research_motivation}
This research is motivated by several key practical and theoretical factors across ubiquitous computing, human-computer interaction, and machine learning:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Democratization and Accessibility of Input Hardware:} Replacing dedicated peripheral hardware with printable paper layouts and standard webcams drastically lowers costs for educational, low-resource, or mobile environments \cite{Khare2019QWERTY, Sreenath2021Monocular}.
    \item \textbf{Customizability for Specialized Tasks:} Industrial, laboratory, cleanroom, and accessibility applications frequently require non-standard keypads, macro triggers, or custom spatial configurations that cannot be achieved with off-the-shelf physical keyboards.
    \item \textbf{Hygienic and Touchless Public Interfaces:} In medical facilities or public kiosks, physical touch surfaces accumulate bacteria and pathogens. Disposable or cleanable paper keyboard sheets paired with camera tracking offer a sterile interaction medium.
    \item \textbf{Advancements in Deep Sequential Learning:} Leveraging modern skeletal keypoint extractors combined with temporal LSTM networks allows complex kinetic motion patterns (such as key press deceleration and contact impact) to be learned directly from scale-normalized joint feature sequences, eliminating fragile hand-crafted heuristic rules \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM, Zhang2020MediaPipe, GilMartin2023Hand, Gammulle2021TMMF, Simao2016Unsupervised, Doan2022Efficient, Nunez2018Convolutional, Lai2018CNN, Ikram2020Skeleton}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Research Aim}
\label{sec:research_aim}
The overarching aim of this research project is to design, implement, and empirically evaluate a customizable, low-latency paper-based virtual keyboard system that utilizes monocular RGB video tracking, AprilTag fiducial homography mapping, scale-normalized landmark processing (without temporal smoothing), and a PyTorch LSTM deep touch classifier to achieve reliable key press detection and spatial input execution on arbitrary flat surfaces.

\section{Research Objectives}
\label{sec:research_text_objectives}
To achieve the primary research aim, the work is structured into four sequential research objectives:

\subsection{1.7.1 To Identify...}
\label{subsec:obj_identify}
To identify and synthesize baseline theoretical principles, planar homography algorithms, skeletal hand tracking models, and feature normalization methodologies from existing literature to define the core requirements for monocular touch detection and customizable paper keyboard interfaces.

\subsection{1.7.2 To Analyze...}
\label{subsec:obj_analyze}
To analyze hand joint kinematic feature representations, scale-normalization relative to unitless hand length, raw feature propagation (without temporal low-pass smoothing), and temporal windowing parameters (5-frame sliding window with 2-frame overlap) for optimizing input feature representations fed into deep temporal neural networks.

\subsection{1.7.3 To Design and Develop...}
\label{subsec:obj_design}
To design, implement, and integrate an end-to-end software and deep learning framework comprising:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A PySide6 desktop GUI designer for interactive keyboard layout creation, XML format layout export (defining key coordinates, action bindings, and marker anchors), and printable PDF generation with embedded AprilTag markers.
    \item An AprilTag visual tracking pipeline for computing real-time $3 \times 3$ Planar Homography matrices ($H$) to correct camera perspective tilt and paper displacement \cite{Wang2016AprilTag2, Kallwies2020Determining}.
    \item A MediaPipe Hand Landmarker pose estimation module integrated with a hand-length scale-normalization processor (operating directly on raw keypoints without temporal smoothing) \cite{Zhang2020MediaPipe, SanchezBrizuela2023Lightweight}.
    \item A PyTorch LSTM deep neural network touch detection classifier trained on 5-frame temporal sliding windows for multi-finger surface impact classification \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM, GilMartin2023Hand, Simao2016Unsupervised}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{1.7.4 To Evaluate...}
\label{subsec:obj_evaluate}
To evaluate the integrated system's touch detection classification accuracy (precision, recall, F1-score), homography spatial transformation mapping accuracy, system latency (FPS throughput), typing responsiveness, and comparative robustness under variable ambient lighting and surface configurations.

\subsection{Detailed Operational Architecture Steps}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Layout Design \& Export:} The user designs a custom key layout using the PySide6 desktop designer application. The application exports two synchronized artifacts: an XML specification defining key region boundaries, button values, action commands, and fiducial marker anchor locations; and a high-resolution printable PDF containing the layout grid embedded with AprilTag fiducial anchors.
    \item \textbf{Physical Setup \& Capture:} The user prints the keyboard layout on standard paper and places it on any flat workspace surface. A single commodity RGB webcam captures the surface video feed.
    \item \textbf{Fiducial Tracking \& Homography Calculation:} The AprilTag detector tracks the four corner fiducial markers on the printed paper, extracting their 2D pixel locations \cite{Wang2016AprilTag2, Kallwies2020Determining, Shabalina2018ARTag, Kalaitzakis2021Fiducial}. A $3 \times 3$ Planar Homography matrix ($H$) is computed to map pixel coordinates from the camera image space directly into the normalized XML layout coordinate space \cite{Pirchheim2011Homography, Herdiansyah2024Implementation, GarridoJurado2026ArUco}.
    \item \textbf{Hand Skeleton Estimation \& Scale Normalization:} MediaPipe Hand Landmarker extracts 21 3D/2D skeletal joint keypoints for detected hands \cite{Zhang2020MediaPipe, SanchezBrizuela2023Lightweight, Sreenath2021Monocular, Ivacko2024Edge, Andriyanov2025Improving}. Raw coordinates are normalized relative to hand scale (unitless wrist-to-middle-mcp distance) to achieve scale and distance invariance. Temporal smoothing filters (such as 1\texteuro\ filters) are omitted to preserve raw temporal dynamics directly for sequence classification.
    \item \textbf{Temporal Feature Windowing:} Scale-normalized joint position and derived velocity vectors are assembled into sliding temporal feature windows of 5 frames with a 2-frame overlap (stride of 3 frames) \cite{Simao2016Unsupervised, Gammulle2021TMMF}.
    \item \textbf{LSTM Touch Classification:} The custom PyTorch LSTM neural network evaluates each 5-frame feature window to classify whether a finger touch event occurred on the physical surface \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM, GilMartin2023Hand, Doan2022Efficient, Nunez2018Convolutional, Lai2018CNN}.
    \item \textbf{Homography Coordinate Mapping \& Key Execution:} Upon confirmation of a touch event, the active fingertip's pixel coordinates ($P_{\text{pixel}}$) are transformed via $H$ into the XML layout coordinate frame ($P_{\text{XML}} = H \cdot P_{\text{pixel}}$). The XML coordinate is mapped to key boundaries, executing the corresponding virtual key press or system command.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Resource Requirements}
\label{sec:resource_requirements}
The hardware and software specifications required to build, train, and execute the proposed customizable paper-based virtual keyboard system are defined below.

\subsection{Hardware Requirements}
\label{subsec:hardware_req}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Monocular Camera:} Commodity USB RGB Webcam or integrated laptop webcam (720p/1080p resolution operating at $\ge 30$ FPS).
    \item \textbf{Host Computing Platform:} Standard PC or Laptop equipped with an x86\_64 or ARM processor (Intel Core i5/i7 or Apple Silicon/AMD equivalent, $\ge 8$ GB RAM).
    \item \textbf{Hardware Acceleration (Optional for Training):} NVIDIA GPU supporting CUDA or Apple Metal for accelerated PyTorch model training.
    \item \textbf{Physical Media:} Standard A4/Letter paper printer and standard flat surface (desk, table).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Software Requirements}
\label{subsec:software_req}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Operating System:} Linux (Ubuntu 22.04 LTS / 24.04 LTS), macOS, or Windows 11.
    \item \textbf{Programming Language \& Package Manager:} Python 3.10+ managed via `uv` package runner.
    \item \textbf{GUI Framework:} PySide6 (Qt for Python) for layout designer UI development.
    \item \textbf{Computer Vision \& Fiducial Libraries:} OpenCV (`opencv-python`), Pupil AprilTag (`apriltag` Python bindings).
    \item \textbf{Pose Estimation Framework:} Google MediaPipe Hand Landmarker (`mediapipe`).
    \item \textbf{Deep Learning Framework:} PyTorch (`torch`, `torchvision`) for LSTM model definition, training, and real-time inference.
    \item \textbf{Document \& Compilation Toolchain:} TeX Live full suite (`pdflatex`, `latexmk`, `biber`) for thesis compilation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Elaboration of Scope Boundaries}
The research explicitly focuses on monocular RGB input streams to maximize low-cost deployment and portability without requiring specialized depth cameras \cite{Posner2012Single, Enkhbat2020HandKey, Sreenath2021Monocular}. Planar homography mapping is restricted to flat 2D surfaces, which match standard paper printouts on desks or tables \cite{Wang2016AprilTag2, Kalaitzakis2021Fiducial}. Multi-finger touch classification is performed using scale-normalized skeletal keypoint features (without 1\texteuro\ temporal low-pass smoothing) processed by a lightweight PyTorch LSTM model \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM, Zhang2020MediaPipe, GilMartin2023Hand}, optimized to run in real time on standard CPUs without demanding dedicated GPU infrastructure at runtime \cite{Doan2022Efficient, Li2021CNN, Lee2022Virtual}. Advanced features such as multi-user simultaneous typing or complex 3D curved surface unfolding are designated as out-of-scope for this project.

\section{Chapter Summary}
\label{sec:chapter_summary}
This introductory chapter has established the fundamental background, problem statement, research questions, motivation, aim, objectives, solution architecture, resource requirements, and project scope for a customizable paper-based virtual keyboard system. 

By replacing costly depth sensors and unstable shadow analysis with a combined pipeline of printable AprilTag fiducial homography tracking \cite{Wang2016AprilTag2, Kallwies2020Determining}, scale normalization (without temporal smoothing) \cite{Zhang2020MediaPipe}, and a PyTorch LSTM deep touch classifier \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM, GilMartin2023Hand}, the research addresses critical gaps in monocular vision input interfaces. The next chapter, Chapter 2 (Literature Review), provides a comprehensive critical assessment of existing virtual keyboard frameworks, hand pose estimation algorithms, planar homography techniques, and deep learning gesture classification models in current literature.
