\section{Overview of the Chapter and Research Context}
\label{sec:chapter_overview}
Remarkable developments have taken place in the field of Human-Computer Interaction (HCI). Although the use of mechanical keyboards in typing continuous prose is quite efficient, the current technological setting requires additional macro keypads and shortcut controls for various uses like hotkeys for developers, video editing keys, and audio keys \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020, Lee2022Virtual}. But the commercial macro controls available are quite expensive (\$150 to \$300), rigid, and tough to sanitize. However, the previous keyboard systems based on cameras could not replace conventional keyboard systems due to some shortcomings such as flat desks do not have any tactile switch travel resulting in high levels of fatigue \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020}.

In order to address the above problem, this project introduces a user-friendly paper-based virtual macro keyboard using a monocular webcam, AprilTag tracker, and PyTorch deep learning. The keyboard serves as an alternative to a shortcuts controller rather than as a universal keyboard for typing. The user designs their own layout using the dedicated software, and prints it on normal paper placed on the desk. Touching of all five fingers is detected in real-time with a PyTorch LSTM network without a GPU and with a latency of $29.09\text{ ms}$ using a normal commodity CPU. Moreover, the shape of the paper sheet does not depend on any activity performed on the computer; thus, one may use the same sheet of paper to design macro keyboards for video editing, programming, and audio mixing.

Section~\ref{sec:problem_background} reviews current hardware and vision systems, Section~\ref{sec:problem_statement} formulates the problem through the IRCA framework, and Sections~\ref{sec:research_question} to~\ref{sec:chapter_summary} detail research questions, motivations, and project scope.

\section{Problem Background}
\label{sec:problem_background}
In the modern age of computerized processes, the use of computers is much more advanced than the mere typing of texts into prose. Programmers, video editors, 3D animators, audio producers, and digital artists require certain key combinations and multi-step commands for efficient work. In order to avoid fatigue caused by pressing the same buttons repeatedly, professionals depend on macro controllers (like the Elgato Stream Deck and Loupedeck) that complement the main keyboard and mouse \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020, Lee2022Virtual}.

Nonetheless, in the real world, there are severe practical and economic pain points associated with physical macro hardware:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Financial Unfeasibility:} The price of dedicated controllers reaches up to \$150 to \$300 and higher. It becomes financially difficult for students, freelance specialists, or independent developers to acquire these physical pads for various programs.
    \item \textbf{Stiff Physical Layout:} There is a fixed arrangement of plastic buttons that cannot be changed in any way when switching between different tasks like video editing and coding.
    \item \textbf{Contamination and Sensitivity of Specialized Fields:} One cannot use liquid antiseptics in sterile clinics, chemical laboratories, or cleanrooms because moisture damages electronic circuits, while electrical micro-sparks can ignite flammable fumes.
\end{enumerate}

In order to deal with the challenges that existed in terms of the cost and flexibility of physical devices, the use of cameras for virtual keyboards was investigated \cite{Zhang2001Visual, Srivastava2012RealTime, Khare2019QWERTY, Ji2018Local}. The main issue with earlier research was that it utilized virtual keyboards as a substitute for actual keyboards for typing. This was problematic because the stationary flat surface does not have mechanical key travel, and continuous typing on the virtual surface leads to finger exhaustion and mistakes \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020}.

Though there has always been an inherent benefit in using paper for macro controllers, it has never been easy to design a macro keypad out of paper utilizing a traditional monocular webcam. A monocular webcam cannot measure depth directly, shadow models fail to work when multiple fingers come close to the table \cite{Thomas2013Camera}, and previous visual systems have been either slow dwell models or expensive types that use GPUs and 3D depth cameras \cite{Lee2019Virtual, Kudale2016RealTime}. It is therefore essential to design a practical paper-based macro controller that runs in real-time on standard commodity CPUs using a simple monocular webcam.

\section{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:problem_statement}
The problem statement for this computing research uses the IRCA model (Ideal, Reality, Consequences, and Aim) as follows:

\subsection{Ideally}
\label{subsec:irca_ideally}
It would be ideal for digital artists, programmers, and computer users to have the ability to take any flat surface on their desk and turn it into a customizable auxiliary macro keyboard without using specialized physical hardware. This is different from a mechanical keyboard used for typing continuous text; instead, this interface gives users access to on-demand macro palettes for shortcuts and system commands. The whole system should work with ordinary paper and a monocular RGB camera, running efficiently on a standard consumer-level processor without requiring GPUs, depth cameras, or additional electronics.

\subsection{Reality}
\label{subsec:irca_reality}
In reality, computer users are hindered by practical and algorithmic limitations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Costly and Stiff Hardware Components:} Commercially available macro controllers cost approximately \$150 to \$300, rendering them expensive for students and individual makers \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020}.
    \item \textbf{Misfitting Purpose of Prior Virtual Keyboards:} Previous attempts tried to reproduce continuous typing keyboards, which caused typing errors and user fatigue on flat desks \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020}.
    \item \textbf{One-Finger Tracking Limitation in Monocular Solutions:} Universal monocular solutions track only one finger at a time \cite{Thomas2013Camera, Posner2012Single, Ji2018Local, Khare2019QWERTY}. Shadow tracking fails when finger shadows overlap, while 2D tracking cannot distinguish depth without 3D depth sensors.
    \item \textbf{Latency and GPU Dependence:} Computer vision systems often need dedicated GPU computing or experience significant delays on commodity CPUs \cite{Enkhbat2020HandKey, Ji2018Local}.
    \item \textbf{Artificial Dwell Periods:} Webcams without depth perception required users to pause their finger for 500 to 1000~ms to register a keystroke \cite{Khare2019QWERTY, Chen2024Gaze}.
    \item \textbf{Environmental Sensitivity:} Shadow and skin-color detection methods break down under diffuse lighting, soft ambient shadows, or across diverse skin tones \cite{Thomas2013Camera, Yue2014Blind}.
    \item \textbf{Fixed Camera Alignment and Paper Movement:} Earlier setups required rigidly mounted overhead cameras; any paper displacement caused complete calibration misalignment \cite{Wang2016AprilTag2, Kallwies2020Determining}.
    \item \textbf{Fixed Layouts:} Most previous virtual keyboards used hardcoded QWERTY layouts without software action multiplexing \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Consequences}
\label{subsec:irca_consequences}
Therefore, the dilemma for digital artists, programmers, and students is choosing between two unfavorable alternatives: spending \$150 to \$300 on hardware macro controllers that are inflexible, or putting up with the drawbacks of earlier vision keyboards that had slow dwell times and single-finger limits. Furthermore, sterile and hazardous environments are left without safe, wipeable input solutions.

\subsection{Aim}
\label{subsec:irca_aim}
To solve this problem, the research focuses on developing a paper-based virtual macro keyboard for application shortcuts, command execution, and macros. The virtual macro keyboard acts as an auxiliary controller instead of a continuous typing keyboard. It uses 21-joint MediaPipe skeletal tracking, unitless hand-length scale normalization ($L_{\text{hand}}$), lightweight PyTorch LSTM sequence classification, and AprilTag planar homography tracking ($H$) to enable real-time ($29.09\text{ ms}$), multi-finger macro interactions on commodity CPUs.

\section{Research Question}
\label{sec:research_question}
To address these technical challenges, the primary research question is formulated as follows:

\begin{quote}
\textit{It is an open research question how a monocular computer vision framework using only a regular RGB camera and plain printed paper can enable accurate, fast, and personalized virtual macro keyboard interaction on flat surfaces, allowing users to define custom shortcuts and trigger actions from a single printed layout executing entirely on standard commodity CPUs without dedicated GPUs or specialized hardware.}
\end{quote}

This primary question is addressed through five secondary research questions (Sub-RQs):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Sub-RQ 1 (Fiducial Marker Selection and Planar Homography Tracking):} Among available visual fiducial markers (AprilTag, ArUco, ARTag, STag), which fiducial marker provides the highest planar homography accuracy and lowest corner jitter under perspective tilt and low camera resolution, while maintaining layout tracking when markers are partially occluded?
    \item \textbf{Sub-RQ 2 (Landmark Subsets and Kinematic Representation):} Which combination of the 21 MediaPipe hand joints and unitless hand-length scale normalization ($L_{\text{hand}}$) methods makes finger touches most discriminable when joint velocities are included without temporal smoothing delays?
    \item \textbf{Sub-RQ 3 (Deep Learning Sequence Architecture Optimization):} How can we achieve optimal classification accuracy (F1-score) using lightweight neural network models (comparing 1D CNN, Attention, ResNet, BiLSTM, and LSTM) operating with an inference latency under 3~ms on a commodity CPU?
    \item \textbf{Sub-RQ 4 (Temporal Windowing and CPU Synchronization):} What temporal sliding window size, stride, and sampling rate capture deceleration impact dynamics reliably at 12~FPS on consumer CPUs without a GPU?
    \item \textbf{Sub-RQ 5 (Layout Decoupling and Action Multiplexing):} How can the physical geometry of the printed layout be decoupled from software semantics to allow dynamic binding of multiple action profiles to the same printed paper sheet without reprinting?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Research Motivation}
\label{sec:research_motivation}
Several practical considerations motivate this research:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Affordable Hardware for Macro Pads:} Commercial macro pads like streaming controllers and editing boards are expensive. Using plain paper, regular webcams, and standard CPUs makes custom macro input affordable for coding, media editing, and office workflows \cite{Khare2019QWERTY, Sreenath2021Monocular}.
    \item \textbf{Flexible and Configurable Macro Keypad:} Users can create custom keypad layouts that trigger application hotkeys, numeric inputs, and commands on a single printed sheet, switching software profiles without reprinting.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Finger Interaction with Single-Hand Optimization:} Whereas earlier monocular vision keyboards restricted users to single-finger tracking, this system supports multi-finger interaction across all five fingers (thumb, index, middle, ring, pinky), optimized for single-handed auxiliary operation alongside a mouse or primary keyboard.
    \item \textbf{Hardware Independence on Commodity CPUs:} Real-time processing on standard CPUs enables the system to run on entry-level computers without dedicated GPUs.
    \item \textbf{Hygienic and Disposable Interfaces:} Paper interfaces tracked with AprilTags allow easy replacement and disposal after use, providing a clean input device for medical clinics, workshops, and chemical laboratories \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020}.
    \item \textbf{Lightweight CPU-Based Deep Learning:} Using a compact PyTorch LSTM sequence model to classify impact dynamics on commodity CPUs provides a strong architectural pattern for real-time edge vision systems \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM, Zhang2020MediaPipe, GilMartin2023Hand}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Research Aim}
\label{sec:research_aim}
The ultimate goal of this research is to design, implement, and evaluate an inexpensive and flexible paper-based virtual macro keyboard for application shortcuts, command execution, and macro workflows. Rather than replacing the primary keyboard, this system serves as an auxiliary controller allowing users to create their own layouts on paper and bind multiple software profiles to the same printed sheet. The entire pipeline operates in real time using a standard commodity CPU and a simple monocular RGB camera without requiring specialized hardware or dedicated GPUs.

The pipeline executes through seven operational steps:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Layout Design and Export:} The user configures key boundaries and assigns actions through PySide6, generating a synchronized XML layout file and print-ready AprilTag PDF file.
    \item \textbf{Physical Setup:} The printed layout on plain paper is placed on a desk and monitored by a regular RGB webcam (see setup photographs in Appendix~\ref{ch:appendix_physical_setup}).
    \item \textbf{AprilTag Tracking and Homography:} Corner fiducials are tracked to compute planar homography matrix $H$, mapping image pixels to layout coordinates \cite{Wang2016AprilTag2, Kallwies2020Determining}.
    \item \textbf{Hand Tracking and Normalization:} MediaPipe extracts 21 hand landmarks, scale-normalized by unitless hand length ($L_{\text{hand}}$) without temporal smoothing \cite{Zhang2020MediaPipe}.
    \item \textbf{Temporal Windowing:} Normalized coordinates and velocities form 5-frame sliding windows with 2-frame overlap at 12~FPS \cite{Simao2016Unsupervised}.
    \item \textbf{CPU LSTM Touch Detection:} A lightweight PyTorch LSTM network evaluates each 5-frame window on the CPU, classifying touch probabilities across all five fingers \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM, GilMartin2023Hand}.
    \item \textbf{Coordinate Mapping and Execution:} Confirmed fingertip touch coordinates map via $H$ into XML coordinates ($P_{\text{XML}} = H \cdot P_{\text{pixel}}$) to execute key presses or scripts.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Resource Requirements}
\label{sec:resource_requirements}

\subsection{Hardware Requirements}
\label{subsec:hardware_req}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Camera Sensor:} A standard USB camera or laptop webcam with regular resolution (360p, 480p, 720p). No specialized depth sensor is needed.
    \item \textbf{Host Computer:} A standard commodity PC or laptop (equipped with quad-core x86\_64 or ARM processor and 4~GB RAM).
    \item \textbf{Graphics Hardware:} No dedicated GPU is required; runtime inference executes entirely on a commodity CPU.
    \item \textbf{Physical Media:} Standard desktop printer using plain A4 or US Letter paper.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Software Requirements}
\label{subsec:software_req}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Operating System:} Linux (Ubuntu 22.04/24.04), macOS, or Windows 11.
    \item \textbf{Runtime Environment:} Python 3.10+ managed with the \texttt{uv} package manager.
    \item \textbf{GUI Framework:} PySide6 (Qt for Python) for the Layout Designer application.
    \item \textbf{Computer Vision:} OpenCV and Pupil AprilTag for real-time frame processing and fiducial tracking.
    \item \textbf{Pose Estimation:} Google MediaPipe Hand Landmarker for 21-joint skeletal extraction.
    \item \textbf{Deep Learning:} PyTorch for LSTM sequence model training and CPU runtime inference.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{In-Scope Technical Boundaries}
\label{subsec:in_scope_boundaries}
As detailed in Table~\ref{tab:project_scope}, the system operates using a standard webcam and plain printed paper without specialized sensors \cite{Posner2012Single, Sreenath2021Monocular}. It detects independent touch contact across all five fingers with $29.09\text{ ms}$ CPU latency for flat-surface, single-hand interaction. Application~1 separates physical geometry from actions, allowing one printed sheet to bind to multiple profiles \cite{Doan2022Efficient}. AprilTag tracking compensates for tilt \cite{Wang2016AprilTag2, Kalaitzakis2021Fiducial}, using unitless ($L_{\text{hand}}$) normalized landmarks at 12~FPS and PyTorch LSTM sequence classification on commodity CPU \cite{Zhang2020MediaPipe, GilMartin2023Hand}.

\subsection{Out-of-Scope Boundaries and Future Extensions}
\label{subsec:out_scope_boundaries}
Continuous prose typing is out of scope due to the lack of physical tactile travel on flat paper. Specialized hardware (ToF depth cameras, stereo pairs, infrared projectors, and wearable gloves) is excluded \cite{Lee2019Virtual, Kudale2016RealTime}. Non-planar surfaces, two-handed bimanual typing, multi-user interaction, force sensing, and language model auto-correction remain deferred to future work.
