\section{Chapter Overview}
\label{sec:chapter_overview}
Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) has evolved rapidly over recent decades, driven by the search for natural, intuitive, and ubiquitous interfaces that transcend the constraints of physical input peripherals. Standard physical input devices, such as mechanical QWERTY keyboards and capacitive touchpads, remain the primary modalities for text entry and command execution in desktop and mobile computing. However, their physical form factor, susceptibility to mechanical wear, high manufacturing overhead, and complete lack of spatial layout adaptability restrict their utility in emerging computing paradigms, ubiquitous computing, cleanroom environments, and personalized accessibility contexts \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020, Lee2022Virtual}.

This research introduces a novel, customizable paper-based virtual keyboard system powered by monocular computer vision, fiducial planar tracking, and deep recurrent neural networks. By combining printable paper layouts containing fiducial marker anchors, monocular vision pose tracking, hand-length scale normalization (without temporal smoothing), dynamic homography transformation, and a custom PyTorch Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) finger touch classification model, the proposed architecture turns any flat physical surface into an interactive input interface using standard commodity RGB webcams.

\section{Problem Background}
\label{sec:problem_background}
The fundamental interaction paradigm between humans and computing machinery has relied predominantly on physical electromechanical keyboards for over half a century. While physical keyboards provide tactile feedback and high typing throughput for proficient typists, their static physical boundaries present significant operational limitations \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020}. Physical keyboards are inherently rigid; their key sizes, layout configurations, and spatial arrangements cannot be dynamically reconfigured to accommodate domain-specific command sets, non-standard language character sets, single-handed input requirements, or assistive configurations for individuals with motor impairments.

To overcome the rigid nature of physical hardware, research in visual computer vision interfaces has investigated projection-based and camera-based virtual keyboards \cite{Zhang2001Visual, Srivastava2012RealTime, Khare2019QWERTY, Ji2018Local}. Early virtual keyboard implementations relied heavily on optical projection units coupled with specialized infrared sensors or time-of-flight depth cameras \cite{Lee2019Virtual, Kudale2016RealTime}. While depth sensors provide explicit Z-axis distance measurements to infer surface contact, they require specialized, costly hardware that prevents widespread deployment on standard mobile devices or commodity PCs. Furthermore, classical single-camera RGB approaches attempted touch detection by analyzing optical shadows projected under user fingertips \cite{Thomas2013Camera, Posner2012Single}. However, shadow-based techniques suffer severe instability under variable ambient illumination, directional light changes, dynamic self-occlusion, and non-uniform background surfaces, resulting in high false-positive keystroke rates and unreliable operational stability \cite{Yue2014Blind}.

Recent advancements in deep learning and real-time hand joint estimation frameworks, such as Google MediaPipe Hand Landmarker, have enabled markerless 2D and 3D hand skeleton tracking directly from commodity RGB video feeds \cite{Zhang2020MediaPipe, Sreenath2021Monocular, GilMartin2023Hand, Ivacko2024Edge, Andriyanov2025Improving}. Skeletal keypoint localization operates robustly across skin pigmentation variations and lighting fluctuations by anchoring predictions to anatomical hand landmark structures rather than fragile skin-color thresholding \cite{SanchezBrizuela2023Lightweight}. However, despite high frame-rate keypoint localization, converting 3D/2D spatial hand joint landmarks into accurate physical surface touch events remains a major technical challenge \cite{Li2021CNN, Lee2022Virtual}. Variations in hand-to-camera distance, hand size differences, dynamic perspective tilt, and the lack of physical tactile feedback make simple thresholding of raw fingertip positions inadequate for detecting deliberate touch actions on flat planes \cite{Maman2023TypeNet, Yoo2026WordLevel}. 

Moreover, existing vision-based input systems often lack a seamless mechanism to map physical surface interactions to custom, user-designed key layouts. Without persistent visual anchors and planar perspective correction, small camera shifts or surface displacements invalidate coordinate mappings, making continuous typing input erratic \cite{Wang2016AprilTag2, Kallwies2020Determining, Shabalina2018ARTag, Kalaitzakis2021Fiducial, Pirchheim2011Homography, Zhang2022DynaKey}. Consequently, there is an urgent need for an integrated framework that unifies custom paper layout design, low-latency fiducial planar calibration, scale-normalized landmark feature extraction (without temporal smoothing), deep learning sequential touch detection, and dynamic homography coordinate transformation.

\section{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:problem_statement}
Despite extensive research into vision-based text entry systems, current virtual keyboard solutions struggle with a critical barrier: they either rely on expensive, specialized hardware (such as 3D depth cameras, Time-of-Flight sensors, infrared emitters, stereo camera pairs, active laser projectors, or wearable data gloves) or demand high-end workstation CPUs and dedicated GPUs processing high-resolution video streams. Consequently, existing approaches fail to deliver an accessible, real-time virtual keyboard that operates using only a single regular commodity RGB camera on standard plain printed paper.

\subsection{General Problem}
\label{subsec:general_problem}
At a high level, traditional computing input hardware relies on fixed physical peripherals that impose substantial economic costs, susceptibility to liquid damage and mechanical wear, hygienic contamination risks in public or medical environments, and physical immutability \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020}. While visual virtual keyboards offer a software-driven touchless alternative, existing implementations predominantly rely on costly depth sensors, active laser projection devices, or specialized motion capture infrastructure \cite{Lee2019Virtual, Pilkov2024Markerless}. Alternatively, deep learning vision models often require dedicated GPU hardware acceleration and high-resolution video inputs to achieve interactive frame rates. This heavy dependency on specialized hardware and compute infrastructure severely undermines the democratization, low-cost portability, and rapid deployability that paper-based virtual interfaces promise.

\subsection{Specific Problem}
\label{subsec:specific_problem}
Within the domain of monocular computer vision and human-computer interaction, developing a virtual keyboard that operates on standard plain printed paper using strictly a single regular RGB camera (such as an ordinary USB webcam or budget laptop sensor) without any specialized hardware presents four fundamental technical challenges:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Monocular Z-Axis Ambiguity on Flat Surfaces Without Depth Hardware:} A single regular RGB camera inherently lacks physical Z-axis depth information. Distinguishing between a finger hovering a few millimeters above a plain printed paper sheet and physical surface contact cannot be achieved through static position thresholds or fragile shadow analysis \cite{Thomas2013Camera, Srivastava2012RealTime}, leading to severe false-positive key actuations.
    \item \textbf{Computational Overhead and Hardware Inaccessibility:} Conventional deep vision models and spatio-temporal architectures require high-performance dedicated GPUs and high-resolution camera feeds ($\ge 1080$p) to achieve real-time responsiveness. Standard consumer CPUs quickly bottleneck when executing unoptimized vision-deep-learning pipelines, causing latency spikes and dropped frames.
    \item \textbf{Sensitivity to Camera Resolution and Distance Variations:} Real-time skeletal pose detectors output raw pixel coordinates that scale unpredictably with camera distance, user hand anatomy, and image resolution \cite{Zhang2020MediaPipe, Kumar2026Hybrid}. Without scale normalization, models trained on specific resolutions or camera heights degrade drastically when deployed on low-resolution or budget camera feeds (e.g., 360p or 480p) \cite{Simao2016Unsupervised}.
    \item \textbf{Perspective Distortion on Planar Paper Surfaces:} When a user types on an ordinary sheet of printed paper, camera perspective tilt, angle variations, and unintended paper movement corrupt fixed coordinate grids \cite{Wang2016AprilTag2, Oscadal2020Improved}, invalidating key hit detections unless dynamic planar homography is continuously maintained \cite{Kallwies2020Determining, Kalaitzakis2021Fiducial}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Research Gap:} A critical research gap exists in creating a unified monocular computer vision pipeline that operates on \emph{standard plain printed paper} using \emph{strictly a single regular RGB camera} (without requiring specialized depth sensors, ToF cameras, stereo rigs, laser projectors, or studio-quality optics), and executes in \emph{real time on standard commodity CPUs without requiring a dedicated GPU} (while remaining GPU-accelerable), maintaining high touch detection accuracy across \emph{regular and low camera resolutions} through AprilTag fiducial homography tracking \cite{Wang2016AprilTag2, Kallwies2020Determining}, unitless hand-length scale normalization \cite{Zhang2020MediaPipe}, and a lightweight PyTorch LSTM deep temporal classifier \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM, Gammulle2021TMMF}.

\section{Research Question}
\label{sec:research_question}
To address the identified research gap, this study is guided by the following primary research question:

\begin{quote}
\textit{How can a monocular vision framework using strictly a single regular RGB camera and plain printed paper enable accurate, real-time virtual keyboard interaction on arbitrary flat surfaces executing entirely on standard commodity CPUs without requiring specialized hardware or dedicated GPUs, across regular and low camera resolutions?}
\end{quote}

Sub-investigations associated with this central question include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item How can scale normalization be formulated on hand joint trajectories to achieve complete camera resolution and distance invariance while preserving crisp kinetic contact deceleration dynamics?
    \item What sliding window duration, stride parameters, and model architecture enable a lightweight deep LSTM network to achieve touch classification F1-scores $\ge 0.95$ while executing in real time at a 12~FPS standard on a standard commodity CPU without requiring a GPU?
    \item How robustly does AprilTag planar homography tracking localize plain printed paper layouts under perspective camera tilt angles and physical paper shifts?
\end{itemize}

\section{Research Motivation}
\label{sec:research_motivation}
This research is motivated by several key practical and theoretical factors across ubiquitous computing, human-computer interaction, and machine learning:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Zero Specialized Hardware Cost \& Extreme Accessibility:} Eliminating all specialized hardware (depth cameras, lasers, wearable gloves) in favor of standard plain printed paper, a single regular RGB webcam, and a standard commodity CPU democratizes digital input for low-resource schools, developing regions, and mobile computing \cite{Khare2019QWERTY, Sreenath2021Monocular}.
    \item \textbf{Hardware Independence Across CPU and Camera Tiers:} Architecting the system to execute in real time on standard consumer CPUs without requiring a GPU—while operating robustly across regular and low camera resolutions (e.g., 360p, 480p, 720p)—ensures that the virtual keyboard can run on legacy laptops and budget hardware.
    \item \textbf{Infinite Customizability via Plain Paper Printing:} Industrial cleanrooms, laboratories, and accessibility settings require tailored macro keypads and task-specific layouts that can be designed in software, printed onto ordinary paper for pennies, and replaced instantly.
    \item \textbf{Sterile and Disposable Medical Interfaces:} In sterile surgical theaters and healthcare wards, physical keyboards harbor pathogens; plain paper sheets printed with AprilTag anchors can be laminated, sanitized with hospital disinfectant, or discarded after single use.
    \item \textbf{Real-Time Temporal Deep Learning on Commodity CPUs:} Proving that lightweight recurrent neural architectures (PyTorch LSTM) can capture micro-kinetic deceleration inflections in real time on standard CPUs without GPU acceleration establishes a new benchmark for accessible vision-based interaction \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM, Zhang2020MediaPipe, GilMartin2023Hand, Gammulle2021TMMF}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Research Aim}
\label{sec:research_aim}
The overarching aim of this research project is to design, implement, and empirically evaluate a customizable, real-time paper-based virtual keyboard system that operates using strictly a single regular monocular RGB camera without any specialized hardware (such as depth cameras, Time-of-Flight sensors, or stereo vision pairs), relying on standard plain printed paper layouts embedded with AprilTag fiducial anchors, and executing in real time entirely on standard commodity CPUs without requiring a dedicated GPU (while remaining GPU-accelerable), robustly accommodating regular and low camera resolutions.

\section{Research Objectives}
\label{sec:research_text_objectives}
To achieve the primary research aim, the work is structured into four sequential research objectives:

\subsection{1.7.1 To Identify...}
\label{subsec:obj_identify}
To identify and synthesize baseline theoretical principles, planar homography algorithms, skeletal hand tracking models, and feature normalization methodologies from existing literature, establishing the foundational constraints for single regular RGB camera operation, plain printed paper tracking, and real-time CPU-only execution without specialized hardware.

\subsection{1.7.2 To Analyze...}
\label{subsec:obj_analyze}
To analyze hand joint kinematic feature representations, unitless hand-length scale normalization, direct feature propagation, and temporal sliding windowing (5-frame window with 2-frame overlap at a standardized 12~FPS processing rate) to achieve complete camera resolution independence and real-time execution on standard commodity CPUs without a GPU.

\subsection{1.7.3 To Design and Develop...}
\label{subsec:obj_design}
To design, implement, and integrate an end-to-end two-application software suite comprising:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A PySide6 desktop GUI designer for interactive keyboard layout creation, XML schema generation, and printable PDF export embedded with AprilTag anchors for standard plain paper printing.
    \item An AprilTag visual tracking pipeline for computing real-time $3 \times 3$ Planar Homography matrices ($H$) using single regular RGB video feeds, correcting perspective tilt and paper shifts across regular and low resolutions \cite{Wang2016AprilTag2, Kallwies2020Determining}.
    \item A MediaPipe Hand Landmarker pose estimation module integrated with a hand-length scale-normalization processor operating directly on raw keypoints without temporal smoothing \cite{Zhang2020MediaPipe, SanchezBrizuela2023Lightweight}.
    \item A lightweight PyTorch LSTM deep neural network touch detection classifier ($< 1\text{ MB}$) trained on 12~FPS 5-frame temporal sliding windows for multi-finger touch contact classification, executing in real time on standard commodity CPUs without requiring a GPU \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM, GilMartin2023Hand, Simao2016Unsupervised}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{1.7.4 To Evaluate...}
\label{subsec:obj_evaluate}
To evaluate the integrated system's touch detection classification accuracy (precision, recall, F1-score), homography spatial transformation accuracy, CPU-only real-time latency and throughput across diverse camera resolutions (360p, 480p, 720p, 1080p), typing speed (WPM), Character Error Rate (CER), and operational robustness under plain printed paper setups.

\subsection{Detailed Operational Architecture Steps}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Layout Design \& Export:} The user designs a custom key layout using the PySide6 desktop designer application. The application exports two synchronized artifacts: an XML specification defining key region boundaries, button values, action commands, and fiducial marker anchor locations; and a high-resolution printable PDF containing the layout grid embedded with AprilTag fiducial anchors.
    \item \textbf{Physical Setup \& Capture:} The user prints the keyboard layout on standard paper and places it on any flat workspace surface. A commodity RGB webcam or low-end camera captures the surface video feed across any native resolution.
    \item \textbf{Fiducial Tracking \& Homography Calculation:} The AprilTag detector tracks the four corner fiducial markers on the printed paper, extracting their 2D pixel locations \cite{Wang2016AprilTag2, Kallwies2020Determining, Shabalina2018ARTag, Kalaitzakis2021Fiducial}. A $3 \times 3$ Planar Homography matrix ($H$) is computed to map pixel coordinates from the camera image space directly into the normalized XML layout coordinate space \cite{Pirchheim2011Homography, Herdiansyah2024Implementation, GarridoJurado2026ArUco}.
    \item \textbf{Hand Skeleton Estimation \& Scale Normalization:} MediaPipe Hand Landmarker extracts 21 3D/2D skeletal joint keypoints for detected hands \cite{Zhang2020MediaPipe, SanchezBrizuela2023Lightweight, Sreenath2021Monocular, Ivacko2024Edge, Andriyanov2025Improving}. Raw coordinates are normalized relative to hand scale (unitless wrist-to-middle-mcp distance) to achieve complete invariance to hand size, camera distance, and image resolution. Raw scale-normalized features are propagated directly to preserve crisp kinetic contact dynamics for sequence classification.
    \item \textbf{Temporal Feature Windowing:} Scale-normalized joint position and derived velocity vectors are assembled at a standardized 12~FPS processing rate into sliding temporal feature windows of 5 frames with a 2-frame overlap (stride of 3 frames) \cite{Simao2016Unsupervised, Gammulle2021TMMF}.
    \item \textbf{LSTM Touch Classification on CPU:} The custom PyTorch LSTM neural network evaluates each 5-frame feature window on a standard CPU to classify whether a finger touch event occurred on the physical surface \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM, GilMartin2023Hand, Doan2022Efficient, Nunez2018Convolutional, Lai2018CNN}.
    \item \textbf{Homography Coordinate Mapping \& Key Execution:} Upon confirmation of a touch event, the active fingertip's pixel coordinates ($P_{\text{pixel}}$) are transformed via $H$ into the XML layout coordinate frame ($P_{\text{XML}} = H \cdot P_{\text{pixel}}$). The XML coordinate is mapped to key boundaries, executing the corresponding virtual key press or system command.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Resource Requirements}
\label{sec:resource_requirements}
The hardware and software specifications required to build, train, and execute the proposed customizable paper-based virtual keyboard system are defined below.

\subsection{Hardware Requirements}
\label{subsec:hardware_req}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Monocular Camera:} Any commodity USB webcam, low-end camera, or integrated laptop webcam across arbitrary resolutions (e.g., 360p, 480p, 720p, 1080p). High resolution or high frame rates are not required.
    \item \textbf{Host Computing Platform:} Standard commodity PC or laptop equipped with an x86\_64 or ARM processor (Intel Core i3/i5, AMD Ryzen, or entry-level equivalent, $\ge 4$ GB RAM). The system is engineered to run CPU-only in near real-time without requiring high-end CPUs.
    \item \textbf{Hardware Acceleration (Entirely Optional):} The runtime system executes live inference entirely on CPU without requiring a GPU. GPU acceleration is optional and not required for deployment or runtime execution.
    \item \textbf{Physical Media:} Standard A4/Letter paper printer and standard flat surface (desk, table).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Software Requirements}
\label{subsec:software_req}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Operating System:} Linux (Ubuntu 22.04 LTS / 24.04 LTS), macOS, or Windows 11.
    \item \textbf{Programming Language \& Package Manager:} Python 3.10+ managed via `uv` package runner.
    \item \textbf{GUI Framework:} PySide6 (Qt for Python) for layout designer UI development.
    \item \textbf{Computer Vision \& Fiducial Libraries:} OpenCV (`opencv-python`), Pupil AprilTag (`apriltag` Python bindings).
    \item \textbf{Pose Estimation Framework:} Google MediaPipe Hand Landmarker (`mediapipe`).
    \item \textbf{Deep Learning Framework:} PyTorch (`torch`, `torchvision`) for LSTM model definition, training, and real-time inference on CPU.
    \item \textbf{Document \& Compilation Toolchain:} TeX Live full suite (`pdflatex`, `latexmk`, `biber`) for thesis compilation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Elaboration of Scope Boundaries}
The research explicitly restricts image acquisition to a single regular monocular RGB camera stream and uses standard plain printed paper sheets to maximize low-cost deployment, portability, and zero marginal hardware cost, eliminating any need for specialized depth hardware \cite{Posner2012Single, Enkhbat2020HandKey, Sreenath2021Monocular}. The software pipeline is intentionally engineered around a 12~FPS processing target to ensure near real-time execution on standard commodity consumer CPUs without demanding dedicated GPU infrastructure at runtime (though GPU acceleration is feasible where available) \cite{Doan2022Efficient, Li2021CNN, Lee2022Virtual}. Furthermore, through unitless hand-length scale normalization, the system eliminates any dependency on high-resolution or studio-grade camera optics, operating robustly across regular and low resolutions (e.g., 360p, 480p, 720p). Planar homography mapping is restricted to flat 2D paper sheets on desks or tables \cite{Wang2016AprilTag2, Kalaitzakis2021Fiducial}. Multi-finger touch classification is performed using scale-normalized skeletal keypoint features processed by a lightweight PyTorch LSTM model \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM, Zhang2020MediaPipe, GilMartin2023Hand}, optimized for standard CPU execution. Advanced features such as multi-user simultaneous typing or complex 3D curved surface unfolding are designated as out-of-scope for this project.

\section{Chapter Summary}
\label{sec:chapter_summary}
This introductory chapter has established the fundamental background, problem statement, research questions, motivation, aim, objectives, solution architecture, resource requirements, and project scope for a customizable paper-based virtual keyboard system. 

By replacing costly specialized hardware (such as depth sensors, ToF cameras, laser projectors, and high-end GPUs) with a single regular RGB camera, standard plain printed paper, AprilTag fiducial homography tracking \cite{Wang2016AprilTag2, Kallwies2020Determining}, unitless scale normalization \cite{Zhang2020MediaPipe}, and a lightweight PyTorch LSTM deep touch classifier running in real time on standard commodity CPUs at 12~FPS \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM, GilMartin2023Hand}, the research addresses critical accessibility and technical gaps in monocular vision input interfaces. The next chapter, Chapter 2 (Literature Review), provides a comprehensive critical assessment of existing virtual keyboard frameworks, hand pose estimation algorithms, planar homography techniques, and deep learning gesture classification models in current literature.
