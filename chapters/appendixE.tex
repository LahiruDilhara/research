\section{Overview of Empirical Benchmark Charts}
\label{sec:appendix_benchmarks_overview}
This appendix provides graphs and charts of the experimental evaluation results discussed in Chapter~\ref{ch:results}. These include comparisons between deep learning models, confusion matrices for each finger, AprilTag homography tracking accuracy at different camera tilt angles, and runtime CPU latency breakdowns.

\section{Model Architecture Accuracy versus CPU Latency}
\label{sec:appendix_model_benchmark_chart}
Figure~\ref{fig:appendix_benchmark_acc} compares the Macro F1-score and CPU inference time for different deep learning models tested in our benchmark. The uni-directional LSTM model using scale-normalized positions and velocities gives the best balance, achieving an F1-score of 0.963 with an inference time of only 2.08~ms on the CPU.

\section{Per-Digit Touch Confusion Matrix}
\label{sec:appendix_confusion_matrix_chart}
Figure~\ref{fig:appendix_confusion_matrix} shows the normalized confusion matrix for our selected PyTorch LSTM model across 5,000 test windows. All five fingers show touch detection rates above 93\%. The index finger has the highest accuracy (99.0\% recall), while the pinky finger is slightly lower because of its smaller movement.

\section{AprilTag Homography Tracking Under Perspective Tilt}
\label{sec:appendix_homography_tilt_chart}
Figure~\ref{fig:appendix_homography_tilt} shows the effect of camera tilt angles (from $0^\circ$ to $75^\circ$) on AprilTag tracking. The corner detection rate stays close to 100\% and the average tracking error stays below 0.42~mm for desk angles up to $60^\circ$. Above $65^\circ$, the perspective tilt causes the error to rise, showing that $0^\circ$ to $60^\circ$ is the best angle range.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.88\textwidth]{figures/fig_homography_tilt_error.png}
\caption{AprilTag corner detection rate and RMS planar reprojection error across camera perspective tilt angles from $0^\circ$ to $75^\circ$.}
\label{fig:appendix_homography_tilt}
\end{figure}

\section{Multi-Threaded Real-Time Pipeline Latency Breakdown}
\label{sec:appendix_latency_breakdown_chart}
Figure~\ref{fig:appendix_latency_breakdown} shows the time taken by each step of the pipeline on an Intel Core i7 CPU. The total processing time is $29.09\text{ ms}$ (about $34.4\text{ FPS}$). This uses only about 35\% of the available 83.33~ms budget at 12~FPS, which means the program runs smoothly in real time without needing a GPU.
