\section{Chapter Overview}
\label{sec:meth_chapter_overview}
Designing, implementing, and testing a customizable paper-based virtual keyboard using a single webcam, planar homography, and deep learning requires a clear and organized research methodology. Building a touch interface that uses a single regular camera without special hardware brings together computer vision, hand movement analysis, real-time software development, and user performance testing \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020, Maman2023TypeNet}.

To guide this work step by step, this chapter presents the overall research framework used in this project. Section~\ref{sec:meth_paradigm} explains the philosophical research paradigm and why a quantitative approach is suitable. Section~\ref{sec:meth_approach} details the deductive approach and the Design Science Research Methodology (DSRM) framework. Section~\ref{sec:meth_strategy} describes the partitioned engineering strategy used to test individual technical components before putting the complete system together. Section~\ref{sec:meth_fact_collection} explains the data collection setup, 12~FPS video resampling, hand scale normalization, and dataset preparation. Section~\ref{sec:meth_workflow} provides an execution workflow table connecting the research objectives to technical activities, software components, and quantitative evaluation metrics. Finally, Section~\ref{sec:meth_summary} summarizes the chapter.

\section{Research Paradigm}
\label{sec:meth_paradigm}
A research paradigm is the basic set of beliefs and philosophical principles that guide scientific investigation \cite{Cui2020Deep}. In computer science and software engineering research, paradigms are generally divided into three main perspectives: Positivism, Interpretivism, and Critical Realism \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020}.

\subsection{Philosophical Categorization and Paradigm Selection}
\label{subsec:meth_paradigm_selection}
Scientific research is based on three main philosophical ideas:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Ontology:} Looks at the nature of reality (whether reality exists independently in the physical world or is created by human thoughts and social agreements).
    \item \textbf{Epistemology:} Concerns knowledge and understanding, defining what counts as valid knowledge and how it can be proven.
    \item \textbf{Axiology:} Relates to values and ethics in research, assessing whether the researcher is an objective observer or influenced by personal perspective.
\end{enumerate}

In computer science and human-computer interaction (HCI), researchers usually consider three main research paradigms:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Positivism (Empiricism):} Assumes an objective physical reality governed by predictable natural and mathematical laws. Knowledge is developed through numerical measurements, controlled laboratory tests, mathematical equations, and statistical analysis. The researcher stays objective.
    \item \textbf{Interpretivism (Constructivism):} Assumes that reality is formed through human experiences and social context. It mostly relies on qualitative data such as open interviews, group discussions, and user observations.
    \item \textbf{Critical Realism:} Recognizes that an objective physical world exists, but acknowledges that human observation of this world is shaped by social context and mental models.
\end{itemize}

For this research project, a \textbf{Positivist (Empirical) Research Paradigm} is selected and applied throughout.

\subsection{Justification of the Positivist Quantitative Paradigm}
\label{subsec:meth_paradigm_justification}
Choosing a positivist empirical paradigm fits the engineering nature of this research project:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Objective Measurement of Physical and Motion Data:} Hand joint movements, camera coordinate transformations, planar homography matrix math ($H \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$), and finger deceleration upon touching paper are physical and mathematical events. Evaluating these components requires exact pixel coordinates, scale ratios, and millisecond latency measurements \cite{Zhang2020MediaPipe, Kallwies2020Determining}.
    \item \textbf{Quantitative Evaluation Metrics:} Testing deep learning touch detection models (\texttt{run\_all.py}) across 22 model variations relies on numerical performance metrics: Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-Score, ROC curves, and inference time on standard CPUs \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM, Doan2022Efficient}.
    \item \textbf{Repeatable Controlled Experiments:} A positivist approach supports controlled, repeatable testing across different camera tilt angles, hand sizes, typing speeds, and room light levels to evaluate system reliability \cite{GilMartin2023Hand, Toshpulatov2024RealTime}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Epistemological Threats to Validity and Mitigation Strategies}
\label{subsec:meth_threats_validity}
To keep the research reliable and scientifically valid, several possible threats were identified and addressed:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Construct Validity:} Addressed by using clear mathematical metrics (F1-score, sub-pixel corner location error, end-to-end frame processing time in milliseconds) instead of subjective user ratings.
    \item \textbf{Internal Validity:} Addressed by testing components in five separate research partitions, making sure changes in model accuracy came directly from feature design and model architectures rather than camera noise.
    \item \textbf{External Validity:} Supported by gathering data from 15 participants with different hand sizes ($L_{\text{hand}}$ between $14.2$~cm and $21.5$~cm) and testing the system across various indoor light levels ($150$~lux to over $2,500$~lux).
    \item \textbf{Reliability:} Supported by writing clear and deterministic data preparation scripts (\texttt{normalize\_landmarks.py}, \texttt{create\_windows.py}) and automated training scripts (\texttt{run\_all.py}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Research Approach}
\label{sec:meth_approach}
Scientific research generally uses one of three logical approaches: Inductive reasoning (building new theories from observations), Deductive reasoning (testing hypotheses derived from established theories), or Pragmatic mixed methods \cite{Cui2020Deep}.

\subsection{Deductive Logical Reasoning Framework}
\label{subsec:meth_deductive_approach}
This study uses a \textbf{Deductive Research Approach}. The research starts from established theories in computer vision, motion analysis, and deep learning:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Planar Geometry and Homography Theory \cite{LonguetHiggins1985Machine, Pirchheim2011Homography}:} Deduce that layout coordinates $\mathbf{P}_{\text{XML}}$ can be calculated from webcam pixel coordinates $\mathbf{P}_{\text{pixel}}$ using a planar homography matrix $H$, as long as four clear AprilTag corner points are detected.
    \item \textbf{Scale Invariance in Perspective Projection \cite{Doan2022Efficient}:} Deduce that dividing hand joint coordinates by the middle MCP hand length $L_{\text{hand}}$ removes differences caused by hand size and distance from the camera $Z$.
    \item \textbf{Sequence Learning with Recurrent Neural Networks \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM}:} Deduce that sliding windows of scale-normalized joint velocities capture the sudden slowdown of a finger hitting paper, allowing an LSTM model to detect touch contact without depth cameras.
\end{enumerate}

These theoretical principles were turned into specific software designs and verified through experimental testing.

DSRM is widely used in computing research, focusing on creating and testing software artifacts to solve practical problems.

The implementation of each of the six DSRM phases in this study is detailed below:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Phase 1: Problem Identification and Motivation:} Identified the research gap in monocular paper keyboards by reviewing 57 prior papers \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020, Thomas2013Camera, Maman2023TypeNet}. Most existing systems require expensive depth cameras, active lasers, or powerful GPUs, while single-camera setups struggled with lighting changes, camera perspective tilt, and rigid key layouts.
    \item \textbf{Phase 2: Define Objectives of Solution:} Defined core system goals: (1) work with a single regular RGB webcam without special hardware, (2) work on plain printed paper, (3) run in real time at a 12~FPS standard on standard CPUs without requiring a GPU (while remaining GPU-accelerable), (4) achieve high touch detection accuracy ($F1 \ge 0.95$) across regular and low resolutions (360p, 480p, 720p), (5) achieve accurate marker corner tracking ($< 0.05$ pixels), and (6) export synchronized printable PDF layouts and XML configuration files.
    \item \textbf{Phase 3: Design and Development:} Built two main software applications:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \emph{Application 1 (Layout Designer):} A PySide6 desktop GUI tool (\texttt{designer/designer\_app.py}) for drawing custom button layouts, placing AprilTag markers automatically on borders, exporting printable PDF files, and saving XML layout definitions.
        \item \emph{Application 2 (Runtime Virtual Keyboard Engine):} A multi-threaded runtime engine combining AprilTag homography estimation ($H \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$), MediaPipe 21 hand joint tracking, scale normalization ($L_{\text{hand}}$), 12~FPS 5-frame sliding window PyTorch LSTM evaluation (\texttt{best\_finger\_touch\_lstm.pth}) on CPU, coordinate mapping ($P_{\text{XML}} = H \cdot P_{\text{pixel}}$), and keypress dispatching.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Phase 4: Demonstration:} Tested the full system on a normal desk using standard paper printed from an office printer, paired with an ordinary webcam, triggering single keystrokes and multi-key commands on a standard desktop CPU.
    \item \textbf{Phase 5: Quantitative Evaluation:} Carried out thorough benchmarks: comparing 22 deep learning models (\texttt{run\_all.py}), measuring CPU latency across resolutions (\texttt{camera\_thread.py}), and testing coordinate mapping accuracy (\texttt{designer/analyzer/main.py}).
    \item \textbf{Phase 6: Communication:} Documented the theory, mathematics, software architecture, and test results across the thesis chapters.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Detailed Partition Specifications and Technological Frameworks}
\label{subsec:meth_partition_details}

\subsubsection{Partition 1: Layout Designer GUI and Homography Simulation Suite}
Located in \texttt{designer/} and \texttt{designer/analyzer/}, this partition focused on layout design and coordinate transformation math:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Designer Application (\texttt{designer\_app.py}):} A PySide6 canvas where users place button boxes on an A4 page ($210 \times 297$~mm), assign button identifiers, configure actions or shell commands, and automatically place AprilTag markers at the four corners.
    \item \textbf{Export Engine:} Generates printable PDF files containing key boundaries with AprilTag markers, along with matching XML files defining key coordinates $(x_{\min}, y_{\min}, x_{\max}, y_{\max})$.
    \item \textbf{Simulation Suite (\texttt{analyzer/main.py}, \texttt{homography\_engine.py}):} Simulated synthetic finger touches under various camera angles ($0^\circ$ to $75^\circ$), verifying that the homography matrix $H$ correctly maps camera coordinates back to XML key areas.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Partition 2: Dataset Creation, Resampling, Landmark Extraction, and Scale Normalization}
Located in \texttt{mediapipeDetector/datacreator/}, this partition processed hand motion videos into clean training data:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Video Resampling (\texttt{resample\_12fps.py}):} Down-sampled video recordings to a uniform 12~FPS rate. This frame rate was chosen to capture finger motion clearly while keeping CPU load light for real-time processing on standard processors without a GPU.
    \item \textbf{MediaPipe 21 Landmark Extraction and Anatomical Groupings:} Extracted 2D pixel coordinates $(x_i, y_i)$ for all 21 hand joints of the active interacting hand in each frame (\texttt{num\_hands=1}). Single-hand tracking was used to avoid visual marker occlusion on A4 paper and to maintain low computational latency on standard CPUs. The landmarks span three functional tiers: terminal fingertips (4, 8, 12, 16, 20) for impact detection; intermediate DIP/PIP knuckle joints for flexion curvature; and MCP knuckles/wrist for the palm reference frame.
    \item \textbf{Scale Normalization (\texttt{normalize\_landmarks.py}):} Shifted coordinates relative to the wrist $\mathbf{P}_0$ and divided by hand length $L_{\text{hand}} = \|\mathbf{P}_9 - \mathbf{P}_0\|_2$, producing unitless coordinates $\mathbf{P}_i^{\text{norm}}$. These values were sent directly without smoothing filters to preserve quick deceleration signals.
    \item \textbf{Velocity Calculation (\texttt{calculate\_velocities.py}):} Computed numerical inter-frame velocity vectors $\mathbf{V}_i(t) = (\Delta x_i / \Delta t, \Delta y_i / \Delta t)$ across consecutive frames to capture sudden impact deceleration spikes.
    \item \textbf{Sliding Window Generation (\texttt{create\_windows.py}):} Created 5-frame temporal windows with a 2-frame overlap (stride of 3 frames), yielding input matrices $\mathbf{X}_W \in \mathbb{R}^{5 \times 84}$ representing the single active hand ($21 \text{ joints} \times 4 \text{ values per joint: } x, y, v_x, v_y$).
    \item \textbf{Quality Filtering (\texttt{filter\_window\_quality.py}):} Removed incomplete or noisy windows where landmarks were missing or had low confidence scores.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Partition 3: Deep Learning Model Benchmarking and Selection}
Located in \texttt{mediapipeDetector/deepLearningModels/}, this partition tested multiple model designs and data views to find the best touch detector:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Master Benchmark Engine (\texttt{run\_all.py}):} An automated cross-validation script testing 22 pure 2D model architecture combinations across five model families (1D CNNs, Attention / Transformers, 1D ResNets, BiLSTMs, and uni-directional LSTMs) across multiple feature inputs (raw pixels, wrist-relative coordinates, scale-normalized positions, velocities, and combined representations).
    \item \textbf{PyTorch Model Selection:} Testing showed that a uni-directional multi-layer PyTorch LSTM model trained on 5-frame normalized position and velocity features achieved the best F1-score ($0.963$) with a compact model size ($0.44$~MB) and ultra-low CPU inference latency ($2.08$~ms), saved as \texttt{best\_finger\_touch\_lstm.pth}.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Partition 4: Real-Time Execution, Latency, and Threading Evaluation}
Located in \texttt{mediapipeDetector/realtimeprocess/}, this partition tested live camera capture and real-time inference speed:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Multi-Threaded Camera Capture (\texttt{camera\_thread.py}):} Ran OpenCV camera reading in a separate thread with a safe queue to prevent frame drops.
    \item \textbf{Inference Manager (\texttt{model\_manager.py}):} Prepared incoming 5-frame arrays and ran the PyTorch LSTM model.
    \item \textbf{Latency Evaluation (\texttt{main\_realtime\_ui.py}):} Measured total per-frame pipeline latency, achieving an end-to-end processing time of $29.09$~ms ($\approx 34.4$~FPS) on a standard CPU.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Partition 5: AprilTag Fiducial Localization and Planar Homography Tracking}
Located in \texttt{aprilTag/}, this partition handled paper surface tracking:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Camera Calibration Engine (\texttt{main.py}):} Estimates the camera intrinsic matrix $K$ and distortion values using checkerboard calibration images.
    \item \textbf{AprilTag Corner Estimator (\texttt{estimater.py}):} Detects AprilTag visual markers placed at layout corners and finds their sub-pixel corner coordinates even under steep camera angles.
    \item \textbf{Real-Time Homography Engine (\texttt{homography.py}):} Solves the $3 \times 3$ Planar Homography matrix $H$ using SVD ($\mathbf{A} \mathbf{h} = \mathbf{0}$) to map fingertip pixels $\mathbf{P}_{\text{pixel}}$ into layout coordinates $\mathbf{P}_{\text{XML}} = H \cdot \mathbf{P}_{\text{pixel}}$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Fact Collection and Data Gathering Mechanisms}
\label{sec:meth_fact_collection}
Collecting reliable experimental data requires structured recording setups, careful labeling, feature extraction methods, and experimental controls.

\subsection{Typing Interaction Dataset Capture Protocol}
To train and evaluate the deep sequential touch detection models, a dataset of multi-finger typing on paper was recorded in a laboratory environment:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hardware Setup:} Video was recorded using standard 1080p monocular RGB webcams mounted on adjustable stands at heights between 35~cm and 65~cm above the desk. Camera tilt angles ranged from $30^\circ$ (slanted view) to $90^\circ$ (top-down view).
    \item \textbf{Lighting Variations:} Video data was recorded under three common indoor lighting conditions: Dim Ambient ($150$~lux), Standard Office ($500$~lux), and Bright Sunlight ($2,500+$~lux).
    \item \textbf{Participant Diversity:} Typing samples were gathered from 15 participants with different hand sizes ($L_{\text{hand}}$ between $14.2$~cm and $21.5$~cm), different skin complexions (Fitzpatrick scale Types I to VI), and various typing habits (from single-finger typing to multi-finger touch typing).
    \item \textbf{Action Gesture Protocol:} Participants performed thousands of deliberate touch events and non-touch hovering movements across all five fingers (Thumb, Index, Middle, Ring, Pinky). Actions included intentional surface taps, hovering over keys, lateral hand adjustments, and resting hands on paper.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Ground Truth Annotation and Temporal Alignment Procedure}
\label{subsec:meth_ground_truth_annotation}
Training supervised deep learning models requires exact alignment between video frames and binary touch labels $\mathbf{y}_t = [y_1, y_2, y_3, y_4, y_5] \in \{0, 1\}^5$ for the five fingers (Thumb, Index, Middle, Ring, Pinky).

Ground truth touch labels were established using a two-step labeling process:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Contact Sensor Synchronization:} Physical paper pads were connected to low-voltage contact sensors, giving precise physical contact timestamps $T_{\text{impact}}$.
    \item \textbf{Frame-by-Frame Video Inspection:} Video recordings were examined frame-by-frame using video annotation tools to confirm that fingertip contact matched the sensor timestamps $T_{\text{impact}}$.
    \item \textbf{Binary Target Labeling:} For every 5-frame sliding window, a binary vector $\mathbf{y} \in \{0, 1\}^5$ was assigned to show which finger or fingers touched the paper during that window.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Feature Vector Pipeline Formulation}
\label{subsec:meth_feature_vector}
For each video frame $t$, MediaPipe extracts 21 2D joint coordinates $\mathbf{P}_i(t) = (x_i(t), y_i(t))$ for $i \in \{0, \dots, 20\}$.

The hand length $L_{\text{hand}}(t)$ is measured from the wrist (Landmark 0) to the middle MCP knuckle (Landmark 9):
\begin{equation}
L_{\text{hand}}(t) = \sqrt{(x_9(t) - x_0(t))^2 + (y_9(t) - y_0(t))^2}
\label{eq:meth_hand_length}
\end{equation}

Unitless scale-normalized joint coordinates $\mathbf{P}_i^{\text{norm}}(t)$ are calculated as follows:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{P}_i^{\text{norm}}(t) = \left( \frac{x_i(t) - x_0(t)}{L_{\text{hand}}(t)}, \frac{y_i(t) - y_0(t)}{L_{\text{hand}}(t)} \right)
\label{eq:meth_norm_coords}
\end{equation}

Numerical velocity vectors $\mathbf{V}_i(t) = (v_{x,i}(t), v_{y,i}(t))$ between consecutive frames are calculated at $\Delta t = \frac{1}{12}$~s:
\begin{equation}
v_{x,i}(t) = \frac{x_i^{\text{norm}}(t) - x_i^{\text{norm}}(t-1)}{\Delta t}, \quad
v_{y,i}(t) = \frac{y_i^{\text{norm}}(t) - y_i^{\text{norm}}(t-1)}{\Delta t}
\label{eq:meth_velocity}
\end{equation}

Combining the normalized position $\mathbf{P}_i^{\text{norm}}$ and velocity $\mathbf{V}_i$ for all 21 joints creates an 84-dimensional feature vector $\mathbf{f}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{84}$ per frame. A 5-frame temporal sliding window stacks these vectors into an input matrix $\mathbf{X}_W \in \mathbb{R}^{5 \times 84}$.

\subsection{Detailed Sub-Phase Execution Breakdown}
\label{subsec:meth_subphase_details}
Each phase of the methodology was executed systematically:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Problem Identification and Relevance Justification (Obj 1):} Established the research gap by studying 57 prior papers across 8 sensing methods, defining the baseline parameters for single-camera paper keyboards.
    \item \textbf{Layout Designer and PDF/XML Exporter (Obj 2):} Built Application 1 Layout Designer (\texttt{designer/designer\_app.py}) using PySide6. The software produces printable PDF sheets with embedded AprilTag markers and matching XML layout definition files.
    \item \textbf{Data Pipeline and Scale Normalization Engine (Obj 3):} Implemented 12~FPS video resampling (\texttt{resample\_12fps.py}), 21 landmark extraction, scale normalization ($L_{\text{hand}}$) without low-pass filtering (\texttt{normalize\_landmarks.py}), velocity calculation (\texttt{calculate\_velocities.py}), 5-frame sliding windowing (\texttt{create\_windows.py}), and quality filtering (\texttt{filter\_window\_quality.py}).
    \item \textbf{Deep Learning Model Benchmarking (Obj 4):} Used \texttt{run\_all.py} to benchmark 22 pure 2D model architecture combinations under 5-fold cross-validation to select the best PyTorch LSTM model (\texttt{best\_finger\_touch\_lstm.pth}).
    \item \textbf{AprilTag Homography Engine (Obj 5):} Developed real-time AprilTag corner detection and SVD homography calculation ($H \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$) in \texttt{aprilTag/}, achieving sub-pixel corner precision.
    \item \textbf{System Assembly and Real-Time Evaluation (Obj 6):} Integrated Application 1 and Application 2 Runtime Engine with multi-threaded camera acquisition (\texttt{camera\_thread.py}), achieving an end-to-end processing time of $29.09$~ms ($34.4$~FPS).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Project Management Methodology and Governance}
\label{sec:meth_project_management}
Managing a research project that involves computer vision, deep learning, GUI development, and planar geometry requires an organized project management framework.

\subsection{Agile Scrum Research Adaptation}
This project used an adapted \textbf{Agile Scrum Methodology} organized around research modules. Work was structured into two-week Sprint cycles:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Sprint Backlog Management:} Technical tasks were organized into user stories in a sprint backlog (for example, "Implement scale normalization and direct feature propagation", "Benchmark 1D CNN vs LSTM models", "Optimize 12 FPS pipeline for CPU performance", "Integrate AprilTag homography matrix").
    \item \textbf{Sprint Reviews and Incremental Demos:} At the end of every two weeks, code modules were tested and checked against numerical goals.
    \item \textbf{Iterative Refinement Loops:} If model accuracy fell below expectations, the workflow returned to feature normalization tuning or hyperparameter adjustments.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Ethical Considerations and Data Privacy Protocols}
\label{subsec:meth_ethics}
Because video recording involves human participants, strict ethical and data privacy practices were followed throughout the research:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Informed Consent:} All 15 participants received information sheets explaining the study and signed voluntary consent forms before video recording.
    \item \textbf{Data Privacy and Anonymization:} Raw video recordings containing participant faces were stored only on secure local drives. Once MediaPipe extracted the 2D joint coordinates, raw video files were permanently removed.
    \item \textbf{Irreversible Feature Data:} The saved landmark dataset contains only unitless floating-point coordinate arrays ($(x_i^{\text{norm}}, y_i^{\text{norm}})$). These coordinate files contain no facial images or personal identifying information, protecting participant privacy.
\end{enumerate}
