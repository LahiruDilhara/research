\section{Chapter Overview}
\label{sec:meth_chapter_overview}
The design, implementation, and empirical evaluation of a customizable paper-based virtual keyboard system powered by monocular computer vision, planar homography tracking, and deep sequential learning require a rigorous, multi-faceted research methodology. Developing markerless, single-camera human-computer interaction (HCI) interfaces presents complex challenges that bridge computer vision perception, kinematic signal processing, real-time software engineering, and quantitative human performance evaluation \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020, Maman2023TypeNet}.

To address these challenges systematically, this chapter establishes the overarching research framework governing the study. Section~\ref{sec:meth_paradigm} defines and justifies the philosophical research paradigm under a positivist, quantitative empirical lens. Section~\ref{sec:meth_approach} details the deductive research approach and the Design Science Research Methodology (DSRM) framework. Section~\ref{sec:meth_strategy} outlines the partitioned modular engineering strategy utilized to evaluate isolated technical components before full end-to-end integration. Section~\ref{sec:meth_fact_collection} details the data collection, temporal resampling, hand landmark scale normalization, and dataset curation procedures. Section~\ref{sec:meth_workflow} presents a comprehensive execution workflow mapping research objectives to specific methods, technical artifacts, and evaluation metrics. Section~\ref{sec:meth_project_management} elaborates on the Agile Scrum management framework, project timeline, risk mitigation matrix, and ethical data privacy protocols. Finally, Section~\ref{sec:meth_summary} summarizes the methodological foundation.

\section{Research Paradigm}
\label{sec:meth_paradigm}
A research paradigm represents the foundational philosophical belief system and theoretical worldview that guides scientific investigation \cite{Cui2020Deep}. In information systems, computer science, and software engineering research, philosophical paradigms are categorized across three main perspectives: Positivism, Interpretivism, and Critical Realism \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020}.

\subsection{Philosophical Categorization and Paradigm Selection}
\label{subsec:meth_paradigm_selection}
Scientific inquiry is structured around three core philosophical pillars:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Ontology:} The branch of philosophy concerned with the nature of reality and existence (whether reality exists objectively outside human consciousness or is subjectively constructed).
    \item \textbf{Epistemology:} The study of knowledge acquisition, defining what constitutes valid, legitimate knowledge and how knowledge can be verified.
    \item \textbf{Axiology:} The study of values and ethics governing research investigation, assessing the role of researcher bias versus objective neutrality.
\end{enumerate}

In computer science and human-computer interaction, research paradigms are broadly categorized across three perspectives:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Positivism / Empiricism:} Asserts an objective physical reality governed by deterministic natural laws. Knowledge is advanced through quantitative measurement, controlled laboratory experimentation, mathematical modeling, and statistical hypothesis testing. The researcher remains an objective observer.
    \item \textbf{Interpretivism / Constructivism:} Asserts that reality is socially constructed through human experiences, subjective interpretations, and contextual meanings. It relies primarily on qualitative methods such as open-ended interviews, focus groups, and participant observation.
    \item \textbf{Critical Realism:} Acknowledges an objective physical reality while recognizing that human perception of reality is mediated by underlying social structures and cognitive mechanisms.
\end{itemize}

For this research project, a \textbf{Positivist (Empiricist) Research Paradigm} is formally selected and applied.

\subsection{Justification of the Positivist Quantitative Paradigm}
\label{subsec:meth_paradigm_justification}
The selection of a positivist empirical paradigm is dictated by the fundamental technical nature of the research problem:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Objective Measurement of Physical and Kinematic Phenomena:} Hand joint motion, spatial coordinate scaling, planar homography matrix transformation ($H \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$), and Z-axis deceleration inflections are deterministic physical and mathematical phenomena. Evaluating these mechanisms requires objective pixel measurements, millimeter scale normalization, and exact millisecond latency timings \cite{Zhang2020MediaPipe, Kallwies2020Determining}.
    \item \textbf{Quantitative Benchmark Metrics:} Evaluating deep temporal touch detection models (\texttt{run\_all.py}) across 22 model architectures relies strictly on objective quantitative performance metrics: Classification Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-Score, Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curves, and Multiply-Accumulate (MAC) operational counts \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM, Doan2022Efficient}.
    \item \textbf{Reproducibility and Controlled Experimentation:} The positivist approach enables controlled, repeatable laboratory experiments under varied camera angles, hand sizes, typing speeds, and illuminance levels to validate system robustness empirical claims \cite{GilMartin2023Hand, Toshpulatov2024RealTime}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Epistemological Threats to Validity and Mitigation Strategies}
\label{subsec:meth_threats_validity}
To preserve empirical rigor within a positivist framework, potential threats to validity must be identified and mitigated:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Construct Validity:} Ensured by defining precise mathematical metrics (F1-score, sub-pixel corner error, end-to-end frame latency in milliseconds) rather than subjective qualitative ratings.
    \item \textbf{Internal Validity:} Mitigated by isolating experimental variables across the five research partitions, ensuring that model accuracy shifts stem strictly from feature engineering and architecture choices rather than uncalibrated camera noise.
    \item \textbf{External Validity:} Enhanced by sampling 15 diverse human participants with varying hand dimensions ($L_{\text{hand}} \in [14.2\text{ cm}, 21.5\text{ cm}]$) and evaluating system performance across indoor lux levels ($150\text{ lux}$ to $2,500+\text{ lux}$).
    \item \textbf{Reliability:} Ensured by publishing deterministic data processing scripts (\texttt{normalize\_landmarks.py}, \texttt{create\_windows.py}) and automated cross-validation model training routines (\texttt{run\_all.py}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Research Approach}
\label{sec:meth_approach}
Scientific investigations employ one of three core logical approaches: Inductive reasoning (building theory from observation), Deductive reasoning (testing hypotheses derived from established theory), or Pragmatic mixed-methods reasoning \cite{Cui2020Deep}.

\subsection{Deductive Logical Reasoning Framework}
\label{subsec:meth_deductive_approach}
This study follows a \textbf{Deductive Research Approach}. The research begins with established theoretical foundations in computer vision, kinematic motion modeling, and sequential deep learning:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Projective Geometry \& Homography Theory \cite{LonguetHiggins1985Machine, Pirchheim2011Homography}:} Deduce that planar target coordinates $\mathbf{P}_{\text{XML}}$ can be mapped from camera pixel coordinates $\mathbf{P}_{\text{pixel}}$ via planar homography $H$, provided four stable AprilTag point correspondences are tracked.
    \item \textbf{Kinematic Invariance under Perspective Projection \cite{Doan2022Efficient}:} Deduce that dividing hand joint coordinate displacements by unitless middle MCP hand length $L_{\text{hand}}$ eliminates scale variance caused by hand size and camera distance $Z$.
    \item \textbf{Sequential Recurrent Neural Learning \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM}:} Deduce that temporal sliding windows of scale-normalized joint velocities capture downward acceleration inflections, enabling deep LSTM classifiers to infer paper surface contact without depth hardware.
\end{enumerate}

These theoretical deductions were formalized into explicit system design hypotheses and empirically evaluated through quantitative bench testing.

The detailed operationalization of the six DSRM phases in this study is expanded below:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Phase 1: Problem Identification and Motivation:} Formulated the research gap surrounding monocular paper virtual keyboards by synthesizing literature limitations across 57 studies \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020, Thomas2013Camera, Maman2023TypeNet}. Highlighted the reliance of prior art on expensive specialized hardware (depth sensors, ToF cameras, active laser projectors, or stereo rigs), heavy GPU compute dependencies, depth ambiguity in single-camera feeds, spatial misregistration under perspective tilt, and the absence of interactive plain-paper layout tools.
    \item \textbf{Phase 2: Define Objectives of Solution:} Defined core quantitative and architectural objectives: (1) operating strictly with a single regular monocular RGB camera without specialized hardware, (2) using standard plain printed paper layouts, (3) achieving real-time execution throughput matching the 12~FPS processing standard on standard commodity CPUs without requiring a GPU (while remaining GPU-accelerable), (4) maintaining touch detection accuracy ($F1 \ge 0.95$) across regular and low camera resolutions (360p, 480p, 720p, 1080p), (5) achieving sub-pixel fiducial corner localization ($< 0.05$ pixels), and (6) exporting synchronized vector PDF/XML layout schemas.
    \item \textbf{Phase 3: Design and Development:} Engineered two core synchronized software application artifacts:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \emph{Application 1 (Layout Designer Suite):} A PySide6 desktop GUI tool (\texttt{designer/designer\_app.py}) enabling drag-and-drop key creation, automated AprilTag anchor positioning along layout borders, vector PDF printing engine for standard plain paper, and XML structural schema exporter.
        \item \emph{Application 2 (Runtime Virtual Keyboard Engine):} A multi-threaded background watch engine integrating real-time AprilTag homography estimation ($H \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$), MediaPipe 21 landmark pose tracking, unitless hand-length scale normalization ($L_{\text{hand}}$), 12~FPS 5-frame sliding window PyTorch LSTM evaluation (\texttt{best\_finger\_touch\_lstm.pth}) on CPU, planar coordinate transformation ($P_{\text{XML}} = H \cdot P_{\text{pixel}}$), and XML keystroke/command dispatching.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Phase 4: Demonstration:} Deployed the integrated two-application suite in a realistic desktop computing environment using standard plain paper sheets printed on commodity office printers, paired with an ordinary webcam, executing both single-key triggers and complex multi-key system shell scripts on a standard CPU.
    \item \textbf{Phase 5: Quantitative Evaluation:} Executed comprehensive empirical benchmarks: automated 22-architecture model evaluation (\texttt{run\_all.py}), multi-threaded CPU latency profiling across multiple camera resolutions (\texttt{camera\_thread.py}), and simulated planar homography spatial verification (\texttt{designer/analyzer/main.py}).
    \item \textbf{Phase 6: Communication:} Documented theoretical foundations, mathematical formulations, system software designs, and quantitative empirical findings in LaTeX thesis chapters.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Detailed Partition Specifications and Technological Frameworks}
\label{subsec:meth_partition_details}

\subsubsection{Partition 1: Layout Designer GUI \& Homography Simulation Suite}
Operating in \texttt{designer/} and \texttt{designer/analyzer/}, this partition validated layout design creation and coordinate mapping math in isolation:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Designer Application (\texttt{designer\_app.py}):} Engineered a PySide6 drag-and-drop canvas allowing users to position rectangular key boundaries on an A4 grid ($210 \times 297\text{ mm}$), assign button IDs, configure keystrokes or system shell commands, and automatically place AprilTag fiducial anchors at four layout corners.
    \item \textbf{Dual Exporter Engine:} Generates vector PDF sheets embedded with AprilTag anchors for physical printing and matching XML specification files defining key bounding boxes $(x_{\min}, y_{\min}, x_{\max}, y_{\max})$.
    \item \textbf{Simulation Suite (\texttt{analyzer/main.py}, \texttt{homography\_engine.py}):} Simulated synthetic finger touch inputs under extreme prospective tilts ($0^\circ$ to $75^\circ$), verifying that SVD-solved homography matrix $H$ correctly maps pixel coordinates back to target XML key regions with sub-millimeter spatial precision.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Partition 2: Dataset Creation, Resampling, Landmark Extraction \& Scale Normalization}
Operating in \texttt{mediapipeDetector/datacreator/}, this partition engineered the sequential data processing pipeline:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Video Sub-Sampling (\texttt{resample\_12fps.py}):} Down-samples raw video recordings to a uniform 12~FPS frame rate. This sub-sampling rate was deliberately chosen to optimize temporal observation density while ensuring the entire vision and deep inference pipeline executes in near real-time on standard commodity CPUs without a GPU, across any camera resolution.
    \item \textbf{MediaPipe 21 Joint Pose Extraction:} Extracts 2D pixel coordinates $(x_i, y_i)$ for 21 anatomical joint keypoints per frame.
    \item \textbf{Unitless Scale Normalization Engine (\texttt{normalize\_landmarks.py}):} Translates keypoints to wrist origin $\mathbf{P}_0$ and divides coordinate displacements by middle MCP hand length $L_{\text{hand}} = \|\mathbf{P}_9 - \mathbf{P}_0\|_2$, producing scale-invariant ratios $\mathbf{P}_i^{\text{norm}}$. Raw scale-normalized coordinates are propagated directly to preserve sharp kinetic surface impact deceleration spikes.
    \item \textbf{Numerical Velocity Derivation (\texttt{calculate\_velocities.py}):} Computes first-order spatial velocity vectors $\mathbf{V}_i(t)$ across consecutive frames.
    \item \textbf{Sliding Window Generation (\texttt{create\_windows.py}):} Constructs sliding temporal windows of 5 frames with 2-frame overlap (stride of 3 frames), producing input matrices $\mathbf{X}_W \in \mathbb{R}^{5 \times 84}$.
    \item \textbf{Quality Filtering (\texttt{filter\_window\_quality.py}):} Filters out corrupted sequence windows containing missing keypoint detections or low landmark confidence scores.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Partition 3: Deep Learning Model Benchmarking \& Selection Engine}
Operating in \texttt{mediapipeDetector/deepLearningModels/}, this partition evaluated deep learning architectures to select the optimal temporal classifier:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Master Benchmark Engine (\texttt{run\_all.py}):} An automated cross-validation benchmarking script evaluating 22 pure 2D deep learning model architecture combinations (uni-directional LSTMs, BiLSTMs, 1D CNNs, 1D ResNets) across diverse feature representations (raw pixels vs wrist-centered vs scale-normalized position vs velocity).
    \item \textbf{Optimal PyTorch Model Selection:} Benchmarks established that a multi-layer PyTorch uni-directional LSTM model trained on 5-frame scale-normalized position and velocity sequences yields the highest classification F1-score ($> 0.95$) with minimal memory footprint ($< 1\text{ MB}$), exported to \texttt{best\_finger\_touch\_lstm.pth}.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Partition 4: Real-Time Execution, Latency \& Threading Evaluation}
Operating in \texttt{mediapipeDetector/realtimeprocess/}, this partition validated live streaming and inference performance:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Multi-Threaded Camera Capture (\texttt{camera\_thread.py}):} Runs OpenCV video capture in a dedicated background thread, buffering incoming frames in a thread-safe queue to eliminate frame drop.
    \item \textbf{Inference Model Manager (\texttt{model\_manager.py}):} Manages PyTorch tensor conversions and executes forward pass inference on incoming 5-frame sliding window arrays.
    \item \textbf{Real-Time Evaluation Suite (\texttt{main\_realtime\_ui.py}):} Evaluates end-to-end execution latency, demonstrating a total per-frame pipeline processing latency of $29.09\text{ ms}$ ($\approx 34.4\text{ FPS}$) on standard desktop CPUs.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Partition 5: AprilTag Fiducial Localization \& Planar Homography Tracking}
Operating in \texttt{aprilTag/}, this partition established visual paper surface tracking:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Camera Calibration Engine (\texttt{main.py}):} Estimates intrinsic camera matrix $K$ and distortion coefficients using checkerboard calibration patterns.
    \item \textbf{AprilTag Corner Estimator (\texttt{estimater.py}):} Detects AprilTag visual markers embedded at layout corners, extracting sub-pixel quad corner coordinates under extreme perspective tilt.
    \item \textbf{Real-Time Homography Engine (\texttt{homography.py}):} Solves the $3 \times 3$ Planar Homography matrix $H$ using SVD ($\mathbf{A} \mathbf{h} = \mathbf{0}$), continuously mapping fingertip pixels $\mathbf{P}_{\text{pixel}}$ into XML layout space $\mathbf{P}_{\text{XML}} = H \cdot \mathbf{P}_{\text{pixel}}$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Fact Collection \& Data Gathering Mechanisms}
\label{sec:meth_fact_collection}
Quantitative empirical validation requires structured data collection protocols covering dataset capture, annotation, feature extraction, and experimental controls.

\subsection{Typing Interaction Dataset Capture Protocol}
To train and benchmark deep sequential touch detection models, a dataset of multi-finger typing interactions on paper surfaces was collected under controlled laboratory conditions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hardware Setup:} Video capture was performed using standard 1080p monocular RGB webcams positioned on adjustable desktop mounts at heights ranging from 35 cm to 65 cm above a flat desk surface. Camera inclination angles varied from $30^\circ$ (steep perspective angle) to $90^\circ$ (overhead top-down view).
    \item \textbf{Illuminance Variance:} Video data was recorded across three distinct indoor illuminance levels: Dim Ambient ($150$ lux), Standard Office ($500$ lux), and Bright Direct Sunlight ($2,500+$ lux).
    \item \textbf{Participant Diversity:} Typing sequences were collected from 15 human subjects displaying diverse hand lengths ($L_{\text{hand}} \in [14.2\text{ cm}, 21.5\text{ cm}]$), skin complexions (spanning Fitzpatrick scale Types I--VI), and typing styles (single-finger hunting to multi-finger touch typing).
    \item \textbf{Action Gesture Protocol:} Participants executed thousands of individual touch events and non-touch hovering movements across all five digits (Thumb, Index, Middle, Ring, Pinky). Actions were categorized into deliberate surface touch impact taps, low-altitude hovering sweeps, lateral sliding movements, and resting pauses.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Ground Truth Annotation and Temporal Alignment Procedure}
\label{subsec:meth_ground_truth_annotation}
Training supervised deep learning models requires precise temporal alignment between hand landmark video frames and ground truth binary touch labels $\mathbf{y}_t = [y_1, y_2, y_3, y_4, y_5] \in \{0, 1\}^5$ corresponding to the five active fingers (Thumb, Index, Middle, Ring, Pinky).

Ground truth touch annotations were established using a dual-sync labeling protocol:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{High-Speed Contact Sensor Sync:} Physical contact paper target pads were wired to low-voltage contact sensors, providing millisecond-accurate physical contact timestamp signals $T_{\text{impact}}$.
    \item \textbf{Frame-by-Frame Video Verification:} Video recordings were manually inspected frame-by-frame using video annotation tools to verify that fingertip pixel contact aligned with sensor timestamps $T_{\text{impact}}$.
    \item \textbf{Binary Target Encoding:} For each 5-frame sliding window, a binary vector $\mathbf{y} \in \{0, 1\}^5$ was assigned, indicating which finger(s) established active paper surface contact during the window duration.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Feature Vector Pipeline Formulation}
\label{subsec:meth_feature_vector}
For each video frame $t$, MediaPipe extracts 21 2D joint coordinates $\mathbf{P}_i(t) = (x_i(t), y_i(t))$ for $i \in \{0, \dots, 20\}$.

Hand length $L_{\text{hand}}(t)$ is computed from wrist Landmark 0 to middle MCP Landmark 9:
\begin{equation}
L_{\text{hand}}(t) = \sqrt{(x_9(t) - x_0(t))^2 + (y_9(t) - y_0(t))^2}
\label{eq:meth_hand_length}
\end{equation}

Unitless scale-normalized joint coordinates $\mathbf{P}_i^{\text{norm}}(t)$ are calculated:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{P}_i^{\text{norm}}(t) = \left( \frac{x_i(t) - x_0(t)}{L_{\text{hand}}(t)}, \frac{y_i(t) - y_0(t)}{L_{\text{hand}}(t)} \right)
\label{eq:meth_norm_coords}
\end{equation}

First-order numerical velocity vectors $\mathbf{V}_i(t) = (v_{x,i}(t), v_{y,i}(t))$ are derived across consecutive frames at $\Delta t = \frac{1}{12}\text{ s}$:
\begin{equation}
v_{x,i}(t) = \frac{x_i^{\text{norm}}(t) - x_i^{\text{norm}}(t-1)}{\Delta t}, \quad
v_{y,i}(t) = \frac{y_i^{\text{norm}}(t) - y_i^{\text{norm}}(t-1)}{\Delta t}
\label{eq:meth_velocity}
\end{equation}

Concatenating scale-normalized spatial position $\mathbf{P}_i^{\text{norm}}$ and joint velocity $\mathbf{V}_i$ for all 21 keypoints constructs a 84-dimensional per-frame feature vector $\mathbf{f}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{84}$. A 5-frame sliding window concatenates feature vectors across time into input matrix $\mathbf{X}_W \in \mathbb{R}^{5 \times 84}$.

\subsection{Detailed Sub-Phase Execution Breakdown}
\label{subsec:meth_subphase_details}
Each phase of the methodology execution workflow was systematically executed:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Problem Identification \& Relevance Justification (Obj 1):} Formulated the research gap by analyzing 57 literature sources across 8 sensing modalities, establishing baseline parameters for single-camera monocular paper input.
    \item \textbf{Layout Designer \& PDF/XML Exporter (Obj 2):} Built Application 1 Layout Designer (\texttt{designer/designer\_app.py}) using PySide6. The software generates vector PDF sheets with embedded AprilTag anchors and matching XML bounding box files.
    \item \textbf{Data Pipeline \& Scale Normalization Engine (Obj 3):} Implemented 12 FPS resampling (\texttt{resample\_12fps.py}), 21 landmark extraction, unitless hand length scale normalization ($L_{\text{hand}}$) without low-pass filtering (\texttt{normalize\_landmarks.py}), velocity computation (\texttt{calculate\_velocities.py}), 5-frame sliding windowing (\texttt{create\_windows.py}), and quality filtering (\texttt{filter\_window\_quality.py}).
    \item \textbf{Deep Learning Model Benchmarking (Obj 4):} Driven by \texttt{run\_all.py}, evaluated 22 pure 2D model architecture combinations under 5-fold cross-validation to select the optimal trained PyTorch LSTM model (\texttt{best\_finger\_touch\_lstm.pth}).
    \item \textbf{AprilTag Homography Engine (Obj 5):} Engineered real-time AprilTag corner detection and SVD homography calculation ($H \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$) in \texttt{aprilTag/}, achieving sub-pixel corner mapping.
    \item \textbf{System Assembly \& Real-Time Evaluation (Obj 6):} Assembled Application 1 and Application 2 Runtime Engine with multi-threaded camera acquisition (\texttt{camera\_thread.py}), achieving an end-to-end latency of $29.09\text{ ms}$ ($34.4\text{ FPS}$).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Project Management Methodology \& Governance}
\label{sec:meth_project_management}
Managing a multi-disciplinary research project spanning computer vision, deep learning, GUI engineering, and mathematical geometry requires an agile, structured management framework.

\subsection{Agile Scrum Research Adaptation}
This project adopted an adapted **Agile Scrum Methodology** optimized for academic research partitions. Development was structured into 2-week Sprint cycles:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Sprint Backlog Management:} Technical tasks were broken down into modular user stories and logged in a sprint backlog (e.g., "Implement scale normalization and direct feature propagation", "Benchmark 1D CNN vs LSTM architectures", "Optimize 12 FPS pipeline for CPU execution", "Integrate AprilTag homography matrix").
    \item \textbf{Sprint Reviews \& Incremental Demos:} At the end of each 2-week sprint, engineered artifacts were compiled, benchmarked, and reviewed against quantitative success criteria.
    \item \textbf{Iterative Refinement Loops:} If model accuracy fell below target thresholds during a sprint evaluation, the pipeline looped back to feature normalization tuning or hyperparameter grid search.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Ethical Considerations \& Data Privacy Protocols}
\label{subsec:meth_ethics}
Because video recording involves human subjects, strict ethical and data privacy standards were enforced throughout the research:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Informed Consent:} All 15 dataset recording participants received detailed information sheets explaining the study's purpose and signed voluntary informed consent forms prior to video capture.
    \item \textbf{Biometric Data Privacy and Feature Anonymization:} Raw RGB video feeds containing participant facial features were processed entirely on isolated, non-networked local storage drives. Once MediaPipe extracted 21 2D landmark coordinates, raw video feeds were permanently deleted.
    \item \textbf{Irreversible Feature Conversion:} The scale-normalized landmark dataset consists exclusively of unitless floating-point coordinate arrays ($(x_i^{\text{norm}}, y_i^{\text{norm}})$). These coordinate matrices contain zero identifiable facial or personal biometric identifiers, ensuring full compliance with General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) standards for privacy-by-design.
\end{enumerate}
