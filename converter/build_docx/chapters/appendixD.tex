\chapter*{Appendix D: Ground-Truth Dataset Annotation Platform}

\section*{D.1 Overview of the Annotation Suite}
\label{sec:appendix_annotator_overview}
This appendix contains the screenshots of the dataset labeling software that we developed in order to label the touches in videos. Properly annotating the touch data on a frame-by-frame basis was essential for training and testing our PyTorch model.

Figure~\ref{fig:appendix_annotator_home} shows the opening screen of the application, where we choose our video files and key layout preferences.

\caption{Dataset annotation tool home screen for video session management.}

\section*{D.2 Frame-by-Frame Skeletal Annotation Interface}
\label{sec:appendix_annotator_workspace}
Figure~\ref{fig:appendix_annotator_main} represents the primary labeling interface. The recorded videos are displayed in the form of frames with a rate of 12~FPS. The 21 hand joints from MediaPipe are shown directly over the hand. We use the buttons on the right to label if each finger (Thumb, Index, Middle, Ring, Pinky) touches the paper or not in each frame.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{figures/data_annotator_main_screen_live_annotation.png}
\caption{Dataset annotator main interface showing 12~FPS frame navigation, MediaPipe skeleton overlay, and per-digit contact labeling.}
\label{fig:appendix_annotator_main}
\end{figure}

\section*{D.3 Rapid Keyboard Shortcuts for Annotation Efficiency}
\label{sec:appendix_annotator_shortcuts}
Figure~\ref{fig:appendix_annotator_shortcuts} illustrates the dialog box for the shortcuts in the annotator. As we had to annotate thousands of frames, we introduced some keyboard shortcuts such as keys 1 to 5 to toggle fingers and the spacebar to jump to the next frame.
