\section{Chapter Overview}
\label{sec:chapter_overview}
The domain of Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) has evolved a great deal over recent years. Developers and researchers have always sought natural, intuitive, and inexpensive ways for humans to interact with computers. Traditionally, physically tangible input devices like mechanical keyboards, membrane keypads, and laptop touchpads have been extensively used for text and command inputs. Though physical keyboards have been in widespread use, issues like fixed shape made out of rigid plastic material, tangible buttons, manufacturing costs, and inability to customize the keys present several problems. Physical keyboards, for instance, are difficult to customize to suit specific user requirements, assistive typing for disabled users, and also the sterile environment of hospitals \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020, Lee2022Virtual}.

In an effort to mitigate some of the limitations presented by the physical keyboards, we propose a customizable paper-based virtual keyboard system in this research project. This system uses one conventional monocular RGB camera, AprilTag markers, and a lightweight PyTorch-based deep learning algorithm. Any arbitrary key layout may be designed and printed with standard desktop printers using a desktop application. Once the paper with the layout is put on a flat surface, it is observed by a normal camera. The hand joint motions are tracked in 2D and 3D space by Google MediaPipe Hand Landmarker. Coordinates of the joints are normalized with respect to the hand scale to ensure accurate detection independent of camera distance and type. A lightweight Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) neural network runs in real time on a commodity computer CPU to detect finger touches on the paper plane. Once a finger touch is detected, planar homography converts pixel coordinates from camera frame to the paper layout coordinate frame to invoke a key press or a command. Significantly, the same physical paper layout may be used for different software profiles (like regular typing, developer shortcuts, audio editing, or terminal commands) without reprinting the paper.

\section{Problem Background}
\label{sec:problem_background}
The physical computer keyboard has been an extensively used form of text input for more than fifty years. Although a physical keyboard can give immediate feedback on its usage that allows a well-trained typist to type faster, there are several limitations imposed by the rigidity of the physical keyboard that make it difficult to fit specific cases \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020}. The physical keyboard is a physical device. Its keys cannot be reconfigured after it has been manufactured, thus preventing a user from customizing his or her keyboard to type nonstandard characters, to do one-handed typing, to set up shortcuts for specific applications, or to accommodate people with hand tremor disorders.

In order to cope with the inflexibility of the physical hardware components, researchers of computer vision and human-computer interaction research area have considered several techniques based on projection and camera-based virtual keyboards \cite{Zhang2001Visual, Srivastava2012RealTime, Khare2019QWERTY, Ji2018Local}. In older solutions of virtual keyboards optical laser projector was used in conjunction with special infrared receiver or Time-of-Flight (ToF) depth camera \cite{Lee2019Virtual, Kudale2016RealTime}. Depth sensors calculate distance from camera to the hand of the user along Z axis. This helps with simplification of touches recognition. However, ToF depth camera and laser projector are costly, consume more power and are not included in standard laptops and desktops. Several earlier studies have attempted to detect touches on the projection plane by means of ordinary RGB camera using fingertip shadow tracking \cite{Thomas2013Camera, Posner2012Single}. It turned out that shadow touch recognition is very vulnerable to room illumination changes, hand movement shadows, changing illumination angles and different table color \cite{Yue2014Blind}.

The recent developments in the field of deep learning have led to the availability of some of the open source solutions like Google MediaPipe Hand Landmarker that can track 21 landmarks on hands using simple RGB videos in real time \cite{Zhang2020MediaPipe, Sreenath2021Monocular, GilMartin2023Hand, Ivacko2024Edge, Andriyanov2025Improving}. As MediaPipe relies on machine learning and learns how the human hand looks like, it is much more precise in terms of tracking joints independently from the skin color and lighting conditions than skin color-based segmentation used in older software \cite{SanchezBrizuela2023Lightweight}. Despite the successful hand tracking, the detection of the exact moment of contact between the finger and a paper sheet using a single standard camera remains difficult \cite{Li2021CNN, Lee2022Virtual}. A standard 2D camera doesn't provide any depth information. Different sizes of hands, varying distance from the hand to the camera, and different camera angles don't allow one to determine the point of contact based on the position thresholds \cite{Maman2023TypeNet, Yoo2026WordLevel}.

Finally, most recent research works on virtual typing are confined to QWERTY keyboards coded in the hard code form, thus offering no option for defining the keyboard interface for the user. Any motion of the paper or changing the angle of the camera would imply that the pixel coordinates won’t allow proper mapping of the keys anymore, leading to the clicks being detected in a wrong place \cite{Wang2016AprilTag2, Kallwies2020Determining, Shabalina2018ARTag, Kalaitzakis2021Fiducial, Pirchheim2011Homography, Zhang2022DynaKey}. For this very reason, it can be stated clearly that there is an evident necessity for a system incorporating custom keyboard layouts, printable paper with fiducial markers, homography tracking in real time, normalized features of hands’ joints, and deep learning touch detection algorithm on computer hardware.


\section{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:problem_statement}
Virtual keyboard solutions at present suffer from a critical problem regarding practicality. Firstly, there is a demand for complex and expensive hardware components, such as 3D depth cameras, time-of-flight sensors, infrared detectors, dual cameras, or laser projectors. On the other hand, the use of high-performance graphic processing units (GPUs) is needed to process high-resolution video streams. Consequently, people using standard laptops and cheap USB webcams cannot work with a live and personalized virtual keyboard on a regular printed sheet of paper.

\subsection{General Problem}
\label{subsec:general_problem}
From an overall view, the first limitation of conventional keyboards and other physical devices is that their replacement cost is high, they are easily affected by the spilling of liquids and gathering of dust particles and, lastly, the matter of hygienic nature for use on public systems or clinical environments \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020}. Although vision-based virtual keyboards offer various advantages such as flexibility, ease of use, and low costs, the current methods depend on specialized depth sensing or projecting units which are not usually owned by users \cite{Lee2019Virtual, Pilkov2024Markerless}. Another limitation of many deep learning vision algorithms is that they need advanced hardware like high-resolution cameras and powerful GPUs to work in real-time.


\subsection{Specific Problem}
\label{subsec:specific_problem}
In relation to monocular computer vision and human-computer interaction, developing an accurate, real-time and flexible virtual keyboard on a paper document using an off-the-shelf monocular RGB camera on a regular CPU is a difficult task as there are various challenges that exist in this area of technology:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Limitation of using single finger and multi-finger tracking:} The conventional monocular camera method of keyboard finger tracking works with just one active finger at any point in time \cite{Thomas2013Camera, Ji2018Local, Srivastava2012RealTime}. Shadow-based methods suffer from shadow occlusion when two or more fingers are moved simultaneously \cite{Posner2012Single}. Consequently, conventional vision systems cannot enable natural typing using all five fingers.
    \item \textbf{Identification of surface contact without depth sensor and hover duration:} It is difficult to distinguish between surface contact and hover without the help of depth sensor or hover duration. A regular RGB camera does not provide any depth information on the Z-axis \cite{Thomas2013Camera, Posner2012Single}. It is tough to determine whether the tip of the finger touches the paper or it just hovers a few millimeters above the paper surface. In addition, the earlier versions of these systems would make the user hover his/her finger on the key for a set amount of time \cite{Khare2019QWERTY, Chen2024Gaze}.
    \item \textbf{Latency in computation on standard computers:} Modern deep learning vision architectures typically need powerful GPU computations to run at real-time speeds, thus having high latency in computation on standard computers. Simultaneous operation of hand tracking and deep sequence modeling on a standard CPU can result in CPU overload and latency \cite{Enkhbat2020HandKey, Ji2018Local}.
    \item \textbf{Sensitivity to lighting, shadows, and skin tones:} A shadow detection and threshold approach cannot work effectively where there is diffuse indoor lighting, multiple light sources, soft hand shadows, different skin tones, and complex backgrounds \cite{Thomas2013Camera, Yue2014Blind}.
    \item \textbf{Perspective distortion, camera angle, and motion of the paper:} When the paper keyboard is placed on a desk, due to the tilt of the camera and motion of the paper, keys start moving within the frame \cite{Wang2016AprilTag2, Oscadal2020Improved}. This leads to inaccurate touch detection without planar homography \cite{Kallwies2020Determining, Kalaitzakis2021Fiducial}.
    \item \textbf{Lack of flexibility in layout and multiplexing of actions:} In the past, standard keyboards and even virtual keyboards followed a fixed layout pattern like QWERTY and actions were hard-coded into them \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020}. There is no system available which allows users to create their own custom layout along with assigning different action sets to the same paper without the need of re-printing it again.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Research Gap:} It appears that there is an obvious research gap in the development of a monocular vision system based solely on standard printed paper along with a normal RGB camera, which can recognize individual touch actions of multiple fingers without artificial delays, run in real time without the need of a dedicated GPU, support customized layout designs and switching of dynamic action profiles using the same printed paper, and ensure accurate touch recognition for both high and low resolution camera images by means of AprilTag homography-based tracking \cite{Wang2016AprilTag2, Kallwies2020Determining}, normalized unitless hand-length scale \cite{Zhang2020MediaPipe}, and simple PyTorch LSTM-based finger touch classification \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM, Gammulle2021TMMF}.

\section{Research Question}
\label{sec:research_question}
In order to bridge this gap in the knowledge, the research question for the current study can be formulated as:

\begin{quote}
\textit{How is it possible for a computer vision system that employs only one regular RGB camera along with common printed paper to allow precise, real-time and customizable interaction through virtual keyboard on any flat surface by letting the users create their own keyboard layout and assigning various actions to the printed piece of paper, all on a commodity CPU and without using any special equipment or a GPU?}
\end{quote}

To address the core research questions in a systematic way, the following five research questions will be analyzed:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Selection of Vision-based Fiducial Markers and Paper Layout Tracking Method:} Which is the vision-based fiducial marker technique like AprilTag, ArUco, ARTag, and STag which will give the right homography because of lesser jitter at the corner due to camera rotation angles and fewer resolution videos, and which is the paper layout tracking with occluded markers?
    \item \textbf{Subsets of hand landmarks and features representation:} What will be the best choice for the subsets of 21 hand landmarks from the MediaPipe dataset, normalized with the unitless scaling according to hand length and joint velocity, for the finger touch contact detection problem?
    \item \textbf{Designing an Optimum Neural Network Architecture for Deep Learning-Based Touch Recognition Sequence Model:} Which will be the optimum neural network architecture between 1D CNN, Attention/Transformer, ResNet, BiLSTM, and LSTM that will give maximum accuracy and F1 score as well as a latency below 3 milliseconds in touch recognition?
    \item \textbf{Sliding window and synchronization of the CPU for real-time processing:} What should the size of the sliding window in time, the time interval, and the sampling rate be in order to obtain the deceleration dynamics at 12 frames per second on CPU-based processors?
    \item \textbf{The Separation of Paper Layout and Multiplexing of Actions:} What can be done in order to separate the layout of the paper and the semantics of the software that will allow us to convert a printed page of paper into different sequences of actions (keyboard input, keyboard shortcuts, command line interfaces, etc.)?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Research Motivation}
\label{sec:research_motivation}
There are certain reasons that make it absolutely necessary for the implementation of this research into human computer interaction and assistive technologies:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Cost-effective and easily available:} Since there is no requirement to use the depth sensor, laser and expensive hardware, this makes the technology affordable for low-cost computers through the use of paper, a regular webcam and a regular computer processor \cite{Khare2019QWERTY, Sreenath2021Monocular}.
    \item \textbf{Designing dynamic layouts using flexible sheets:} A keypad for numbers, games, or even a handicap-friendly keypad can be designed based on personal preference and can be printed on a single piece of paper. But the same single piece of paper can be programmed with different software profiles without the need to print another single piece of paper.
    \item \textbf{Independence of hardware for standard processors and low-cost cameras:} The capability to run the real-time performance on the normal consumer processor without needing the graphics processing unit for the low resolution camera feed like 360p and 480p makes the application able to run on old computers as well.
    \item \textbf{Paper based cost effective interface:} Keyboards customized to work on paper for industrial work areas, chemistry labs, and classroom use are easily produced and easily replaceable in case of damage or contamination.
    \item \textbf{Hygienic input for sterilized setting:} Keyboards which can easily collect germs inside the hospital can be replaced with paper keyboards which contain AprilTags either laminated for cleaning purposes or even disposable.
    \item \textbf{Deep Learning in Real Time with Normal CPUs:} Use of PyTorch lightweight LSTM model which is able to detect finger impact dynamics in real time in a CPU without a GPU is a good design pattern for lightweight vision systems \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM, Zhang2020MediaPipe, GilMartin2023Hand, Gammulle2021TMMF}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Research Aim}
\label{sec:research_aim}
The objective of this project is to develop a configurable paper based virtual keyboard system which will allow configuring personalized keyboards by means of paper along with the configuration of the same paper for performing various functions such as keystrokes, shortcuts and shell commands. This operates solely based on the utilization of just one monocular RGB camera, with no other devices needed, according to the simple paper-based approach using AprilTag markers, even in real-time, on normal computer systems without the need for a GPU.

\section{Research Objectives}
\label{sec:research_text_objectives}
This research has been broken down into four objectives to achieve the research objective:

\subsection{To Identify Baseline Requirements and Theoretical Foundations}
\label{subsec:obj_identify}
The investigation into theories, planar homography methods, hand tracking approaches, and feature normalization procedures from various references, in order to set benchmarks for single RGB camera tracking, paper homography, decoupled action profile mapping, and real-time CPU computations without hardware aid.

\subsection{To Analyze Hand Kinematics and Feature Normalization}
\label{subsec:obj_analyze}
The evaluation criteria include consideration of kinematics hand joint properties, normalization of hand length so that it is dimensionless, no intermediate transformation during feature propagation, and sliding window of 5 frames with 2 frames overlapping each other per second with a 12 FPS frame rate. This offers freedom from the resolution of the camera and the processing speed of the CPU without requiring the use of a GPU.

\subsection{To Design and Develop the Two-Application Suite}
\label{subsec:obj_design}
The process of developing the two applications in this software package will be undertaken as described below:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Application 1 of PySide6 is made for the interactive designing of the layouts, generating the XML files to represent the keyboard layout, and creating the sheets containing AprilTags in PDF format on normal papers.
    \item The name of this tool is Runtime Configuration and Command Mapper GUI (Application 2, Component A). This tool allows the user to select the standard RGB web camera input streams, import the layout XML files, assign buttons with keys or shell commands and switch between various action sets of the same printed paper layout.
    \item Vision block using April tags to generate the 3x3 homography matrix in real-time considering only one input stream that is generated by the camera to solve the perspective angle and movement of the paper issue \cite{Wang2016AprilTag2, Kallwies2020Determining}.
    \item The hand keypoints were tracked using the MediaPipe Hand Landmarker tracking model, which used the hand scale length normalizer. No temporal filtering was done on these keypoints \cite{Zhang2020MediaPipe, SanchezBrizuela2023Lightweight}.
    \item Tiny LSTM based PyTorch model for finger touch detection which is of small size less than 1 MB and trained using 12FPS and 5 frames per sequence without using GPU \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM, GilMartin2023Hand, Simao2016Unsupervised}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{To Evaluate System Performance and Latency}
\label{subsec:obj_evaluate}
Performance evaluation of the complete system with respect to the accuracy of touch detection (precision, recall, and F1 score), accuracy of homography mapping, reliability of action dispatching using multi-profiles for a single printed design, latency of real-time CPU usage at different camera resolutions like 360p, 480p, 720p, and 1080p and typing speed in WPM, character error rate and usability on plain paper.

\subsection{Detailed Operational Architecture Steps}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Key Layout Design and Exporting:} User designs layout of keys using PySide6 GUI design tool. This design tool creates two files that work in sync; one is an XML file that has details about location of key regions, button values, commands and anchor points of AprilTags, while the second file is a print layout that is created in PDF format.
    \item \textbf{Paper Document Format and Capture:} The user puts the paper copy of the PDF document onto the table. The video of the image is then recorded using a regular webcam or even the webcam of the laptop.
    \item \textbf{AprilTag Detection and Calculation of the Planar Homography Matrix:} In the module of AprilTag detection, the four fiducial marks located at the four corners of the paper are detected. The coordinates of these four fiducials in 2D pixels are then determined \cite{Wang2016AprilTag2, Kallwies2020Determining, Shabalina2018ARTag, Kalaitzakis2021Fiducial}. The 3 by 3 matrix $H$, which is used in the transformation from camera pixels to XML coordinates, is determined \cite{Pirchheim2011Homography, Herdiansyah2024Implementation, GarridoJurado2026ArUco}.
    \item \textbf{Hand Tracking and Scaling:} The MediaPipe hand landmarker has 21 landmarks, where these landmarks are represented by coordinates available in the hand \cite{Zhang2020MediaPipe, SanchezBrizuela2023Lightweight, Sreenath2021Monocular, Ivacko2024Edge, Andriyanov2025Improving}. The scaling of these landmarks is done based on the size of the hands, which in turn is determined from the distance between the wrist and the metacarpophalangeal joint of the middle finger. Normalized raw scale feature value is fed directly into the classifier without smoothing in time domain in order to maintain deceleration peak value.
    \item \textbf{Temporal Windowing:} Joint positions and their corresponding velocities are sampled at 12 FPS through temporal windows of 5 frames each, with 2 frames overlap between adjacent windows (step size of 3 frames) \cite{Simao2016Unsupervised, Gammulle2021TMMF}.
    \item \textbf{CPU-Based LSTM Touch Detection:} The LSTM neural network created for use with PyTorch distinguishes between touch and no touch based on each five-frame sequence on the CPU \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM, GilMartin2023Hand, Doan2022Efficient, Nunez2018Convolutional, Lai2018CNN}.
    \item \textbf{Transformation of Coordinates by Homography and Key Recognition:} As soon as the touch action is detected, the coordinates of the pixel corresponding to the finger that touches the screen are converted into the coordinates of the XML layout according to the matrix $H$ and are compared with the boundaries of keys. This executes the given keyboard shortcut or executes the given command using the shell.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Hardware Requirements}

\subsection{In-Scope Technical Boundaries}
\label{subsec:in_scope_boundaries}
As far as the hardware and the sensor variety is concerned, the research employs one simple RGB webcam and regular printed papers \cite{Posner2012Single, Enkhbat2020HandKey, Sreenath2021Monocular}. The system can be operated by using budget USB webcams as well as laptop webcams that have either normal or low resolution, i.e., 360p, 480p, 720p, and 1080p. High-speed cameras, professional lenses, and depth sensors are not needed at all. The keyboard pattern is printed on regular A4/Letter papers via an ordinary inkjet or laser printer on the office computer desk. There are neither any electronics nor any sensors embedded in the paper. Moreover, the algorithm is implemented in real-time without any GPU usage.

Regarding the design for interaction and vision tracking, the current design allows the interaction and tracking of one hand with 21 landmarks of the active hand. In this scenario, the deep learning algorithm calculates the probabilities of touching of all five fingers of the active hand including Thumb, Index, Middle, Ring and Pinky finger in one stroke. The interaction with the single hand not only allows the reduction of latency up to 29.09 ms but also avoids the occlusion of the four fiducial AprilTag border anchors on the small A4 sheets of paper. The decoupling of physical paper shape and digital action semantics is achieved using the PySide6 Layout Designer allowing the user to configure his key layout and use the same physical sheet of paper to make different kinds of actions such as typing, developer shortcuts, DAW music controls, and terminal commands by switching configurations in the software \cite{Doan2022Efficient, Li2021CNN, Lee2022Virtual}. The vision tracking is done using the AprilTag planar homography tracking which compensates for the paper movement and camera angle \cite{Wang2016AprilTag2, Kalaitzakis2021Fiducial}. The hand landmarks are normalized to the hand length with no temporal smoothing of 12 FPS and classified using the compact PyTorch LSTM neural network on CPU \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM, Zhang2020MediaPipe, GilMartin2023Hand}.

\subsection{Out-of-Scope Boundaries and Future Extensions}
\label{subsec:out_scope_boundaries}
To make this particular design feasible and inexpensive enough to be implemented in normal PCs, some advanced features have been left out purposefully in this design. Firstly, with respect to the hardware side of things, there is no use of any sensors like 3D depth sensors, Time-of-Flight camera, stereo cameras, Infrared laser pointer, and sensor gloves \cite{Lee2019Virtual, Kudale2016RealTime}. Neither has the use of e-paper and ink circuit been considered in this design. Typing on 3D surfaces like cylinder and spheres has been left out of consideration as well. Only typing on normal paper in a flat plane has been taken into account in this case. Secondly, regarding the interaction aspect of this design, two-handed typing and multi-user interaction have not been considered in this design as well. Two-handed detection will confuse the AprilTag marker detection from the normal A4 papers and also cause CPU overload. Finally, 3D force detection of the fingers, touch pressure calculation, and auto-completion of texts using language models have not been considered in this design.

Above is the introductory chapter which covers the background of the problem, the problem statement, research questions, motivation, objective, aim, solution architecture, resource requirements, and scope of the custom paper virtual keyboard project.

By employing a cheap alternative such as an RGB camera, a sheet of paper, AprilTags planar homography tracking \cite{Wang2016AprilTag2, Kallwies2020Determining}, unit scale normalization \cite{Zhang2020MediaPipe}, and the light-weight PyTorch LSTM touch classifier operating at 12~FPS on commodity hardware \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM, GilMartin2023Hand}, this study manages to tackle some of the most critical issues of accessibility and technological complexity associated with the monocular vision-based user interface technologies.
