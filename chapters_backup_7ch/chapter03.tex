\section{Research Paradigm}
\label{sec:meth_paradigm}
Paradigm of Research is described as the philosophy and principle used as the base of scientific research \cite{Cui2020Deep}. In Computer Science and Software Engineering research, there are three paradigms and these include Positivism, Interpretivism, and Critical Realism \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020}.

\subsection{Philosophical Categorization and Paradigm Selection}
\label{subsec:meth_paradigm_selection}
Philosophy of Science is composed of the following three philosophies:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Ontology:} It is related to the nature of reality where reality can be either real and existent in physical world or constructed through the process of human thoughts.
    \item \textbf{Epistemology:} It is related to knowledge and cognition where knowledge is true and how the truth is verified.
    \item \textbf{Axiology:} It is related to ethical considerations in science and whether the scientist is objective in making observations or subjective because of his point of view.
\end{enumerate}

There are three main paradigms in computer science and human-computer interaction research:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Positivism (Empiricism):} The paradigm assumes the existence of an objective reality which operates according to natural and mathematical laws. Knowledge can be acquired through numbers, laboratory research, mathematics and statistics. Researchers are objective.
    \item \textbf{Interpretivism (Constructivism):} The paradigm assumes the non-existence of objective reality since there exists only socially constructed reality through individual experience. Qualitative techniques are used (open interviews, focus groups, user observations).
    \item \textbf{Critical Realism:} The paradigm assumes the existence of objective reality and the fact that people's perception of reality depends on their social environment and minds.
\end{itemize}

In the present study, a \textbf{Positivist (Empirical) Research Paradigm} will be applied throughout the research process.

\subsection{Justification of the Positivist Quantitative Paradigm}
\label{subsec:meth_paradigm_justification}
Empirical Paradigm based on Positivism is an appropriate methodology to use in the engineering character of the current research:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Measurement of Physical and Movement Phenomena:} The movement of hand joints, conversion of coordinates in cameras, calculation of the planar homography matrix ($H \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$), and deceleration of fingers during their touch on paper, are physical and mathematical events. Estimation of these phenomena requires exact measurement of pixel coordinates, scale ratios, and latency in milliseconds \cite{Zhang2020MediaPipe, Kallwies2020Determining}.
    \item \textbf{Numerical Criteria of Evaluation:} Performance tests of 22 various touch detection models (\texttt{run\_all.py}) on the basis of deep learning need such numerical criteria of evaluation as accuracy, precision, recall, F1-Score, ROC curves, and CPU inference time \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM, Doan2022Efficient}.
    \item \textbf{Replicability of the Experiments:} The paradigm of positivism can enable one to conduct replicable experiments under various angles of inclination of the camera, different sizes of hands, typing speed, and lighting conditions of the room \cite{GilMartin2023Hand, Toshpulatov2024RealTime}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Epistemological Threats to Validity and Mitigation Strategies}
\label{subsec:meth_threats_validity}
There are several threats to validity and reliability of the research which were considered in advance in order to ensure high scientific quality of the research:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Construct Validity:} Threat is overcome by application of objective mathematical measures (F1-score, sub-pixel corner location error, total computation time per frame in milliseconds) instead of subjective user feedback.
    \item \textbf{Internal Validity:} Threat is overcome by checking each part of the model across five independent partitions and ensuring that any changes in model performance are caused only by feature engineering and model structure.
    \item \textbf{External Validity:} This threat is overcome by performing the experiment on 15 people with different hand sizes ($L_{\text{hand}}$ between $14.2$~cm and $21.5$~cm) and different indoor lighting levels ($150$~lux to over $2,500$~lux).
    \item \textbf{Reliability:} Threat is overcome by developing deterministic algorithms and scripts for data preparation (\texttt{normalize\_landmarks.py}, \texttt{create\_windows.py}) and model training (\texttt{run\_all.py}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Research Approach}
\label{sec:meth_approach}
Scientific research in scientific communities normally takes place in accordance with one of the three paradigms of logic: Induction (formulation of theories based on observations), Deduction (testing of hypotheses based on proven theories) or Pragmatic (approach which combines both of these paradigms) \cite{Cui2020Deep}.

\subsection{Deductive Logical Reasoning Framework}
\label{subsec:meth_deductive_approach}
In the current study, a \textbf{Deductive Research Approach} was chosen. The theoretical background of the research lies in well proven theories in the areas of computer vision, motion analysis and deep learning:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Planar Geometry and Homography Theory \cite{LonguetHiggins1985Machine, Pirchheim2011Homography}:} The deduction is that layout coordinates $\mathbf{P}_{\text{XML}}$ can be derived from webcam pixel coordinates $\mathbf{P}_{\text{pixel}}$ using planar homography matrix $H$ provided that at least four AprilTag corners are detected.
    \item \textbf{Scale Invariant Perspective Projection Theory \cite{Doan2022Efficient}:} The deduction is that dividing coordinates of hand joints by the middle MCP hand length $L_{\text{hand}}$ will lead to cancelling out all the differences caused by hand size and distance to the camera.
    \item \textbf{Sequence Learning with Recurrent Neural Networks Theory \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM}:} The deduction is that sliding windows of normalized joint velocities will allow capturing the fast slowdown of the finger movement when touching the paper, making it possible to detect the touch contact without depth cameras.
\end{enumerate}

The theoretical background mentioned above was put into practice through implementation into the software.

All six DSRM methodology phases used in this study are described below in the given order:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Phase 1: Problem Identification and Motivation:} The gap in monocular paper keyboards was identified according to the review of 57 papers in this field \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020, Thomas2013Camera, Maman2023TypeNet}. Most existing systems require expensive depth cameras, active laser beams, or high-performance GPUs, while single-camera setups suffered from lighting changes, camera perspective tilt, and fixed layout of the keyboard keys.
    \item \textbf{Phase 2: Define Objectives of Solution:} Objectives of system development were defined as follows: (1) capability of working with a single regular RGB camera without any additional hardware; (2) ability to use the system on normal printed paper; (3) real-time operation at 12~FPS standard on standard CPUs without requiring a GPU (while remaining GPU-accelerable); (4) touch detection accuracy ($F1 \ge 0.95$) across regular and low resolutions (360p, 480p, 720p); (5) marker corner detection with accuracy ($< 0.05$~pixels); and (6) synchronization of printable PDF layouts and XML configuration files.
    \item \textbf{Phase 3: Design and Development:} Two software applications were developed:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \emph{Application 1 (Layout Designer):} PySide6 graphical user interface application for designing custom button layouts, automatic placement of AprilTags on layout borders, generating printable PDF files, and storing XML files with layout definitions (\texttt{designer/designer\_app.py}).
        \item \emph{Application 2 (Runtime Virtual Keyboard Engine):} Multi-threaded engine for real-time operation with AprilTag homography estimation ($H \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$), MediaPipe 21 hand joint tracking, scale normalization ($L_{\text{hand}}$), 12~FPS 5-frame sliding window PyTorch LSTM evaluation (\texttt{best\_finger\_touch\_lstm.pth}) on CPU, coordinate mapping ($\mathbf{P}_{\text{XML}} = H \cdot \mathbf{P}_{\text{pixel}}$), and keypress dispatching.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Phase 4: Demonstration:} Carried out test runs on the system for the complete setup involving a normal table, normal paper printed by an office printer, and an ordinary webcam for triggering one or more keystrokes and shell commands on a standard desktop CPU.
    \item \textbf{Phase 5: Quantitative Evaluation:} Benchmarking done for 22 different deep learning model architectures (\texttt{run\_all.py}), benchmarking CPU latency across various resolutions (\texttt{camera\_thread.py}), and testing coordinate mapping accuracy (\texttt{designer/analyzer/main.py}).
    \item \textbf{Phase 6: Communication:} The theory, mathematics, software architecture, and test results are documented across the thesis chapters.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Detailed Partition Specifications and Technological Frameworks}
\label{subsec:meth_partition_details}

\subsubsection{Partition 1: Layout Designer GUI and Homography Simulation Suite}
The partition is placed in the following directories, \texttt{designer/} and \texttt{designer/analyzer/}, with a focus on layout designing and coordinate transform calculations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Designer Application (\texttt{designer\_app.py}):} A PySide6 application where button boxes can be drawn on an A4 paper ($210 \times 297$~mm), naming buttons, associating actions or shell commands with them, and drawing AprilTags at all four corners.
    \item \textbf{Export Engine:} Produces printable PDFs together with the AprilTags and boundary keys, and an XML file with key coordinates $(x_{\min}, y_{\min}, x_{\max}, y_{\max})$.
    \item \textbf{Simulation Suite (\texttt{analyzer/main.py}, \texttt{homography\_engine.py}):} Conducted synthetic touch simulations from various camera angles ($0^\circ$ to $75^\circ$) to test the correctness of the homography matrix $H$ used in the camera coordinate to XML key region transform.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Partition 2: Dataset Creation, Resampling, Landmark Extraction, and Scale Normalization}
The part of the pipeline that converts the video frames of hand movements into clean training data is located at \texttt{mediapipeDetector/datacreator/}. The following actions were undertaken to create the clean training data:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Video Frame Resampling (\texttt{resample\_12fps.py}):} Resamples the video frames into 12~FPS. The frame rate is chosen according to the possibility to capture the finger movements while maintaining minimum CPU load during the process on non-GPU regular processors.
    \item \textbf{MediaPipe 21 Landmarks Detection and Anatomical Grouping:} Detects the 2D pixel positions $(x_i, y_i)$ of 21 landmarks in the interacting hand per frame (\texttt{num\_hands=1}). The tracking of only one hand permits the prevention of visual markers' occlusion by the A4 paper as well as reduces computation latency on the standard CPU. The landmarks are grouped into three functional layers: terminal fingertips (4, 8, 12, 16, 20) for impact detection; intermediate DIP/PIP joints for flexion curvature detection; and MCP knuckles/wrist for palm reference frame detection.
    \item \textbf{Scale Normalization (\texttt{normalize\_landmarks.py}):} Transforms the landmark coordinates with respect to the wrist joint $\mathbf{P}_0$ and scales the landmark coordinates relative to hand length $L_{\text{hand}} = \|\mathbf{P}_9 - \mathbf{P}_0\|_2$. Provides unitless values $\mathbf{P}_i^{\text{norm}}$ without any filtering for capturing deceleration of the finger upon impact.
    \item \textbf{Velocity Estimation (\texttt{calculate\_velocities.py}):} Inter-frame velocity vector estimation $\mathbf{V}_i(t) = (\Delta x_i / \Delta t, \Delta y_i / \Delta t)$ for capturing deceleration spikes of the finger upon impact.
    \item \textbf{Sliding Window Creation (\texttt{create\_windows.py}):} Creates sliding windows of 5 frames with 2-frame overlap (stride of 3 frames), providing input matrices $\mathbf{X}_W \in \mathbb{R}^{5 \times 84}$ for the single active hand ($21 \text{ joints} \times 4 \text{ values per joint: } x, y, v_x, v_y$).
    \item \textbf{Quality Check (\texttt{filter\_window\_quality.py}):} Excludes invalid windows according to landmark presence and confidence scores.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Partition 3: Deep Learning Model Benchmarking and Selection}
The sub-component that resided in \texttt{mediapipeDetector/deepLearningModels/} implemented several architectures and viewpoints of the data to find the best touch detector architecture:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Master Benchmark Engine (\texttt{run\_all.py}):} This was an automated engine that performed cross-validation by testing 22 different 2D architectures on five different kinds of architectures (1D Convolutional Neural Networks, Attention/Transformer, 1D ResNet, BiLSTM, and uni-directional LSTM) using multiple feature inputs (pixel value, position of wrist joint, normalized position and velocities).
    \item \textbf{PyTorch Model Selection:} It came to the conclusion from the tests conducted that a uni-directional multi-layer LSTM using the features of the normalized position and velocity of 5-frame yields the best F1-Score ($0.963$), having an extremely small model size of $0.44$~MB and fast inference time on the CPU ($2.08$~ms), all of which are stored in \texttt{best\_finger\_touch\_lstm.pth}.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Partition 4: Real-Time Execution, Latency, and Threading Evaluation}
Under the \texttt{mediapipeDetector/realtimeprocess/} directory, the below-mentioned component tested the performance in real-time of live video capture and inference rate:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Multi-threading Video Capture (\texttt{camera\_thread.py}):} Video capture through OpenCV in a separate thread using the thread-safe queue to avoid any dropped frames.
    \item \textbf{Inference Manager (\texttt{model\_manager.py}):} 5-frame arrays were used as input and the PyTorch LSTM model was run over them.
    \item \textbf{Latency Testing (\texttt{main\_realtime\_ui.py}):} The latency of the pipeline was calculated for every frame and the processing time of $29.09$~ms ($\approx 34.4$~FPS) was found for a common CPU.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Partition 5: AprilTag Fiducial Localization and Planar Homography Tracking}
The above-mentioned partition can be found in \texttt{aprilTag/} and consists of the paper surface tracking as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Camera Calibration Engine (\texttt{main.py}):} This module calculates the intrinsic matrix $K$ and distortion coefficients estimation of the camera with the help of calibration images of the checkerboard.
    \item \textbf{AprilTag Corner Estimator (\texttt{estimater.py}):} This module identifies AprilTag visual markers located at the layout corners and estimates their sub-pixel corner locations regardless of a large tilt angle of the camera.
    \item \textbf{Homography Engine (\texttt{homography.py}):} This module computes the homography matrix $H \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ using SVD ($\mathbf{A} \mathbf{h} = \mathbf{0}$) for the plane to calculate fingertip pixel coordinates ($\mathbf{P}_{\text{XML}} = H \cdot \mathbf{P}_{\text{pixel}}$).
\end{itemize}

\section{Fact Collection and Data Gathering Mechanisms}
\label{sec:meth_fact_collection}
The accurate gathering of data needs to have proper organization of the recording, labeling of data, feature extraction, and experimentally controlled conditions.

\subsection{Typing Interaction Dataset Capture Protocol}
In the case of training and testing of deep learning touch detection models, a set of experiments was performed in a properly controlled environment:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hardware Setup:} Video data was captured by standard 1080p monocular RGB webcams that were installed on stands at the height of 35~cm to 65~cm above the desk surface. The camera viewing angles are in the range of $30^\circ$ to $90^\circ$.
    \item \textbf{Lighting Conditions:} There were three lighting conditions chosen for video experiments that represent most common conditions in the room: Dim Ambient ($150$~lux), Typical Office ($500$~lux), and Bright Sunlight ($2,500+$~lux).
    \item \textbf{Participants:} Typing was performed by 15 people with different hand sizes ($L_{\text{hand}}$ between $14.2$~cm and $21.5$~cm), skin types (Fitzpatrick scale Types I to VI), and typing abilities (from single-finger typing to multi-finger touch typing).
    \item \textbf{Actions:} 15 participants were instructed to perform actions including touching and hovering with five fingers (Thumb, Index, Middle, Ring, Pinky). These actions include tap gestures, hovering, hand positions, and resting on paper.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Ground Truth Annotation and Temporal Alignment Procedure}
\label{subsec:meth_ground_truth_annotation}
The training of supervised deep learning models involves complete matching between the video frames and the binary labels $\mathbf{y}_t = [y_1, y_2, y_3, y_4, y_5] \in \{0, 1\}^5$ associated with the touch events of the five fingers (Thumb, Index, Middle, Ring, Pinky).

The ground truth labels of the touch events were obtained using a two-phase labeling procedure which consisted of the following steps:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Contact Sensor Synchronization:} The paper pads were connected to low-voltage contact sensors for obtaining accurate contact timestamps $T_{\text{impact}}$.
    \item \textbf{Video Frame-wise Analysis:} The video frames were analyzed to synchronize the touch events with the obtained timestamps through video annotation tools.
    \item \textbf{Binary Labeling of Targets:} Binary vectors $\mathbf{y} \in \{0, 1\}^5$ were created for each window of five video frames to determine which fingers made contact with the paper.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Feature Vector Pipeline Formulation}
\label{subsec:meth_feature_vector}
Each frame $t$ is processed, which yields 21 pairs of coordinates of the joints in 2D space $\mathbf{P}_i(t) = (x_i(t), y_i(t))$ for $i \in \{0, \dots, 20\}$.

The distance from the wrist (the 0-th landmark) to the middle MCP joint (the 9-th landmark) represents the length of the hand $L_{\text{hand}}(t)$:
\begin{equation}
L_{\text{hand}}(t) = \sqrt{(x_9(t) - x_0(t))^2 + (y_9(t) - y_0(t))^2}
\label{eq:meth_hand_length}
\end{equation}

The normalized coordinates of the joints without any units $\mathbf{P}_i^{\text{norm}}(t)$ can be determined using the formula provided below:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{P}_i^{\text{norm}}(t) = \left( \frac{x_i(t) - x_0(t)}{L_{\text{hand}}(t)}, \frac{y_i(t) - y_0(t)}{L_{\text{hand}}(t)} \right)
\label{eq:meth_norm_coords}
\end{equation}

The vectors of velocities of numerical type $\mathbf{V}_i(t) = (v_{x,i}(t), v_{y,i}(t))$ for each two adjacent frames, which are processed with 12~FPS ($\Delta t = \frac{1}{12}$~s), are calculated:
\begin{equation}
v_{x,i}(t) = \frac{x_i^{\text{norm}}(t) - x_i^{\text{norm}}(t-1)}{\Delta t}, \quad
v_{y,i}(t) = \frac{y_i^{\text{norm}}(t) - y_i^{\text{norm}}(t-1)}{\Delta t}
\label{eq:meth_velocity}
\end{equation}

The combination of the normalized coordinates $\mathbf{P}_i^{\text{norm}}$ and velocities $\mathbf{V}_i$ of 21 joints represents the 84-dimensional feature vector $\mathbf{f}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{84}$ that can be constructed into an input matrix $\mathbf{X}_W \in \mathbb{R}^{5 \times 84}$ using a 5-frame sliding window.

\subsection{Detailed Sub-Phase Execution Breakdown}
\label{subsec:meth_subphase_details}
Methodology was carried out in the following manner:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Problem Identification and Relevance Justification (Objective 1):} Define the research gap based on the analysis of 57 papers regarding 8 types of sensing technologies, which allows defining baseline parameters for paper keyboard using a single camera.
    \item \textbf{Layout Designer and PDF/XML Exporter (Objective 2):} Implementation of Application 1 (Layout Designer) using PySide6 (\texttt{designer/designer\_app.py}). This application allows exporting printed sheets in PDF format containing AprilTags as well as XML layout files.
    \item \textbf{Data Pipeline and Scale Normalization Engine (Objective 3):} It consists of 12~FPS video resampling (\texttt{resample\_12fps.py}), 21 landmarks detection, scale normalization ($L_{\text{hand}}$) without low-pass filter (\texttt{normalize\_landmarks.py}), velocity estimation (\texttt{calculate\_velocities.py}), 5 frames sliding window (\texttt{create\_windows.py}) and quality filter (\texttt{filter\_window\_quality.py}).
    \item \textbf{Deep Learning Model Benchmarking (Objective 4):} Perform the automatic benchmarks (\texttt{run\_all.py}) for choosing the most appropriate model architecture among 22 architectures of pure 2D models using 5-fold cross-validation with PyTorch LSTM (\texttt{best\_finger\_touch\_lstm.pth}).
    \item \textbf{AprilTag Homography Engine (Objective 5):} AprilTag corner detection as well as SVD-based homography calculation ($H \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$) in \texttt{aprilTag/} with sub-pixel precision.
    \item \textbf{System Assembly and Real-Time Evaluation (Objective 6):} Assembly of Application 1 and Application 2 Runtime Engine using multi-threaded camera capturing (\texttt{camera\_thread.py}) and end-to-end processing time of $29.09$~ms ($34.4$~FPS).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Project Management Methodology and Governance}
\label{sec:meth_project_management}
The planning of research with respect to aspects of computer vision, deep learning, graphical user interface design, and plane geometry is one of those which require very effective coordination in project management.

\subsection{Agile Scrum Research Adaptation}
In this ongoing research, the agile scrum process of management was adapted in terms of research modules. The process is described as below:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Sprint Backlog:} Technical issues were categorized in terms of user stories for each sprint (for example: Scale normalization, Benchmark CNN and LSTM models, 12~FPS Pipeline optimization for CPU performance, AprilTag Homography Matrix).
    \item \textbf{Sprint Review and Demonstration:} Research coding module was analyzed on numerical grounds for each two weeks.
    \item \textbf{Iteration Refining Loops:} If accuracy of the model was not up to the mark, then the model had to be worked upon in terms of feature normalization or hyperparameter optimization.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Ethical Considerations and Data Privacy Protocols}
\label{subsec:meth_ethics}
As video recordings involve the presence of humans, the following are the ethics and data protection measures which have been strictly followed throughout the entire experiment:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Consent:} Information sheets were provided to each of the 15 participants and they were made to sign consent forms before starting the video recording procedure.
    \item \textbf{Data Protection and Anonymity:} The raw video clips containing the faces of the participants were kept safely on the secured local hard disk. After extracting 2D landmarks using MediaPipe, the raw video data was removed from the local hard disk.
    \item \textbf{Permanence of Data Extracted:} The final extracted data consists only of the floating point array ($(x_i^{\text{norm}}, y_i^{\text{norm}})$) with only coordinates of the participants and not any image or any other data of the participants.
\end{enumerate}
