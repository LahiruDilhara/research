\section{Chapter Overview}
\label{sec:lit_chapter_overview}
Due to constant innovation in the field of mobile computing technology, augmented and virtual reality, as well as computer systems being widely implemented into everyday objects, our approach towards interacting with computers has changed as well. For almost the entire period of the last century, the text input and generally computer interaction have been carried out using QWERTY mechanical keyboards and capacitive touch screens. Despite the fact that the mechanical keyboard allows the user to receive tangible tactile feedback and hence type quickly, the lack of mobility and fixation of the mechanical keyboard poses some challenges \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020, Lee2022Virtual}. First of all, the mechanical keyboard occupies the permanent space on the desk, is not flexible to changes of the software, can lead to infections in hospital wards or public kiosks, and is easily damaged due to liquid spills and dust accumulation \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020}.

In order to overcome this problem, a number of attempts have been made by the computer vision experts to create virtual input devices that can transform any two-dimensional plane into an input surface using the ordinary camera sensor \cite{Zhang2001Visual, Srivastava2012RealTime, Khare2019QWERTY, Maman2023TypeNet}. The virtual keyboard could be created using the method called monocular vision, which looks very promising because it uses a planar surface monitored only by one RGB camera without any additional switches or electric board components.

Nevertheless, the creation of an accurate, efficient, and adjustable virtual keyboard through a usual RGB video stream does not lack the challenges associated with computer vision, gesture recognition, and sequence learning. They are the following challenges:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Detection of Touch on Surface without Using Depth Sensors:} Monocular cameras with RGB color filters do not give any indication regarding depth along the Z-axis. The challenge arises in identifying the exact moment when the finger actually touches the surface in order to differentiate between pressing a button and touching the paper above the surface \cite{Thomas2013Camera, Posner2012Single, Lee2019Virtual, Toshpulatov2024RealTime}.
    \item \textbf{Hand Size and Signal Distortion:} In the hand landmark-based model, the output of the pixel coordinate is dependent on the size of the hand, the distance from the camera, and the position of the user \cite{Zhang2020MediaPipe, Doan2022Efficient}. Normalizing the spatial landmarks without applying temporal filtering, which would cause distortion of touch events, is crucial for detecting touch events \cite{Kumar2026Hybrid}.
    \item \textbf{Camera Tilt and Plane Orientation:} Even a slight misalignment between the paper and webcam alignment will lead to distortion of the perspective. This, in turn, makes the coordinate grids fail. It is therefore necessary that the planar markers and homography matrix ($H \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$) are computed in real time \cite{Wang2016AprilTag2, Kallwies2020Determining, Pirchheim2011Homography, Herdiansyah2024Implementation}.
    \item \textbf{Custom Design of Layouts and Mapping of Actions:} All virtual typing programs currently available use a standard QWERTY keyboard, but none of them allow for creating custom layouts in written form, allowing certain buttons to be assigned to particular keystrokes or shell commands \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020}.
\end{enumerate}

An in-depth critical review of the literature in vision-based virtual input, planar marker tracking, hand joint estimation, feature scaling, and deep learning sequence modeling will be discussed in this chapter. The way the literature has been organized is captured in the concept map provided in Section~\ref{sec:lit_conceptual_map}. In Section~\ref{sec:lit_domain_overview}, there is a brief overview of the field, which includes the history of text input, typing mechanism, ergonomics, and driving forces within the field. Section~\ref{sec:lit_existing_systems} provides the analysis of the available virtual keyboards using eight different kinds of sensors. The description of some basic algorithms and their processing technique is given in Section~\ref{sec:lit_technological_analysis}. The weaknesses of the existing approaches are highlighted in Section~\ref{sec:lit_reflection}.

\section{Domain Overview}
\label{sec:lit_domain_overview}
The two major channels in human-computer interaction are text entry and commands. Despite the many advancements that have been made in technology, the traditional QWERTY keyboard is still the primary method used for typing on the computer \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020}. The keyboard makes use of tactile sensation and click sounds, which facilitate speedy typing, but there are many disadvantages associated with the traditional keyboard. The physical keyboard is a fixed device that occupies physical space on the desk. The physical keyboard is incapable of being used for other purposes such as audio mixing, video editing, CAD design, or assistive typing, and it collects dust and germs in office and hospital environments.

\subsection{Historical Evolution of Text Entry and Spatial HCI Hardware}
\label{subsec:lit_history_evolution}
Text Entry and Spatial Human-Computer Interaction Technologies have seen four generations of technology in their development journey till date:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Old Typewriter Keyboards, Mechanical and Electrical Keyboards (1870 to 1980):} The first keyboards that were used on the typewriters as well as personal computers had mechanical and electrical keys with lever and contact switch respectively \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020}. The QWERTY keyboard is a layout that was never designed to increase typing speed; instead, it was developed to prevent typebar jamming on early typewriters.
    \item \textbf{Membrane Keyboards and Touch Screen (1990s to 2000s):} A rubber membrane keyboard was used in personal computers to minimize the manufacturing cost while a capacitive touchscreen was the input device for smartphones and tablets \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020}. A capacitive touchscreen enabled the use of multi-touch flexible software keyboard which provided no tactile feedback at all.
    \item \textbf{Laser Projected and Light Curtain Infrared Keyboards (2000s to 2010s):} Laser projection keyboards made use of miniature laser projectors that would project the keyboard pattern on a flat surface like the table while the infrared light curtain sensor would detect finger action \cite{Cheng2015Fingertip, Kudale2016RealTime}. Although portable, they were very power hungry and did not provide any tactile feedback.
    \item \textbf{Monocular Vision, Hand Detection and Tracking, and Deep Learning (After 2010):} Real-time deep learning-based systems such as MediaPipe can be used to achieve 3D hand tracking via 21 joint points through the use of RGB cameras \cite{Zhang2020MediaPipe, GilMartin2023Hand, Maman2023TypeNet}. Using planar homography tracking and a light-weight sequence model, it is possible to achieve the personalized virtual keyboard through papers.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Kinematic Typing Dynamics and Biomechanical Surface Impact}
\label{subsec:lit_typing_biomechanics}
Kinematic principles, which describe the workings of finger movements, are vital in ensuring that tactile sensing is done accurately \cite{Simao2016Unsupervised, MacRitchie2015Integrating, Pilkov2024Markerless}. There are four kinematic phases of key pressing:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Approaching Stage ($t_1$ to $t_2$):} With an increase in the rate of approach of the finger towards the key, there is an increase in the speed of movement downwards ($V_y$).
    \item \textbf{Impact Stage ($t_2$ to $t_3$):} Physical contact between the finger and the paper takes place. Within an incredibly short period of time of under 40 milliseconds, the velocity becomes zero ($V_y \to 0$), causing a high peak in the rate of deceleration ($A_y = \frac{dV_y}{dt} \ll 0$).
    \item \textbf{Resting Stage ($t_3$ to $t_4$):} It is at this stage when the finger temporarily rests on the surface of the paper while the touch event is registered.
    \item \textbf{Recoil Phase ($t_4$ to $t_5$):} The finger moves upward against the paper surface to return to its initial position, where vertical velocity changes its direction ($V_y > 0$).
\end{enumerate}
As one types on the paper placed on the table surface, the physical boundary created by the rigid surface causes a sudden reduction in velocity of motion. However, as one types in mid-air interface, the lack of physical boundary leads to overshooting of fingers \cite{Boletsis2019TextInput}.

\subsection{Ergonomics, Fitts' Law, and Human Input Performance Metrics}
\label{subsec:lit_fitts_law}
Typing rate, accuracy, and pointing are quantitatively evaluated by the HCI metrics for the typing interface type:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Words Per Minute (WPM):} The typing speed will be determined using the formula whereby the number of characters minus one is divided by the total time taken to multiply by five: $\text{WPM} = \frac{|T| - 1}{S} \times 60 \times \frac{1}{5}$, where $|T|$ is the number of typed characters and $S$ is elapsed time in seconds.
    \item \textbf{Keystrokes Per Character (KSPC):} This is basically the relationship between the total number of keystrokes performed, including the corrections made, and the number of characters finally produced. Physical keyboards have a Keystrokes per Character of around 1.05 while mid-air keyboards have more than 1.30 because of the false triggers.
    \item \textbf{Model for Fitts' Law:} It is a measurement of the time taken to move the hand and hit the target key which has width $W$ and is situated at distance $D$ from the present position of the hand:
    \begin{equation}
    MT = a + b \log_2 \left( 1 + \frac{D}{W} \right) = a + b \cdot ID
    \label{eq:fitts_law}
    \end{equation}
    where $ID$ is the Index of Difficulty measured in bits. Customized Paper Virtual Keyboards can be useful in making adjustments of the size of keys $W$ in the software itself, thereby decreasing the value of $ID$ (Index of Difficulty).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Physical Surface Interaction Paradigms: Desk vs. Paper vs. Floating Air}
\label{subsec:lit_surface_paradigms}
In comparison between physical and digital surface interfaces, there can be noted differences in terms of ergonomic and sensory perceptions because of three major paradigms:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Flat Desk Surface:} Provides a tangible boundary that prevents movement of hands and fingers, but no visual boundaries exist unless they are marked out or projected through optical equipment \cite{Srivastava2012RealTime}.
    \item \textbf{Printed Sheet of Paper:} Utilizes tactile medium together with distinct printed keys and AprilTags \cite{Zhang2001Visual, Khare2019QWERTY}. Consumes zero power, involves no optical display latency and no visual distraction.
    \item \textbf{Floating Air Space:} For this form of touch technology, there is no need for any surface at all; nevertheless, this will lead to tiredness of the arms, which is known as "Gorilla Arm Syndrome" \cite{Boletsis2019TextInput, Enkhbat2020HandKey}. Further, this type of touch technology provides no feedback to the user.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Biomechanical Kinematic Modeling of Multi-Finger Tendon Coupling}
\label{subsec:lit_biomechanical_coupling}
The anatomic finger tendon coupling is present in human beings, where the flexor tendons are attached to consecutive fingers, e.g., middle and ring fingers \cite{Simao2016Unsupervised, Pilkov2024Markerless}.

When the user presses a key using his or her active finger, some involuntary actions of the other fingers occur in the vicinity of the action. However, the heuristic method based on single fingers makes the mistake of assuming that these involuntary actions are actual key-pressing actions \cite{Thomas2013Camera, Srivastava2012RealTime}, while deep learning models can actually distinguish between involuntary actions and actual key-pressing actions.

\subsection{Domain Drivers for Customizable Paper Virtual Keyboards}
\label{subsec:lit_domain_drivers}
There are three realistic reasons in the physical world for the creation of a virtual keyboard on paper:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Low-Cost Accessibility:} Virtual keyboards enable computer accessibility through the low-cost interface made possible with the use of low-cost webcams and normal printer paper in the areas of education, home computing and developing nations \cite{Khare2019QWERTY, Sreenath2021Monocular}.
    \item \textbf{Macros for Customization and Simultaneous Actions on One Key:} Programmers, sound technicians, and digital artists have the ability to customize their keyboards in addition to being able to program more than one set of actions onto one key \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020}.
    \item \textbf{Sterile Environment for Medical and Laboratory Workspaces:} Conventional physical keyboards can be dirty and germy in terms of sterile environments such as laboratories and clinics; however, paper-based keyboard sheets are easily disposed of \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Existing Systems, Frameworks, and Sensing Modalities}
\label{sec:lit_existing_systems}
To examine the existing status of the virtual keyboard, previous virtual keyboards are analyzed with eight kinds of sensors.

\subsection{Projection-Based and Infrared Sensor Systems}
\label{subsec:lit_proj_systems}
The virtual keyboards that work on the principle of projection technology use optical projection and either an infrared screen or infrared camera \cite{Kudale2016RealTime, Cheng2015Fingertip}. Laser keyboards are an example of projection technology where the keyboard keys are projected onto a surface using red projection. At the same time, an infrared light plane is projected a few millimeters above the surface. You can interact with the keys by touching the surface with your fingers \cite{Cheng2015Fingertip}.

Kudale \& Wanjale's method depended on using an infrared webcam with an infrared filtering camera and reflective pens, using four-point planar homography for projection of keys to the coordinates of the surface \cite{Kudale2016RealTime}. The interactive system invented by Cheng et al. \cite{Cheng2015Fingertip} required a projector and a camera, in which hands were separated from the background using geometric calibration, estimating touch by tracking the distance between a fingertip and its shadow.

While the process can be carried out using regular tables and without any need for paper at all, there are quite a few restrictions on the hardware used:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Ambient Light Sensitivity:} Exposure to bright sunlight or even normal office lighting is sufficient to cause the disappearance of the laser pattern and interferes with infrared sensors, causing tracking failures.
    \item \textbf{Requirements for Power Supply and Cost:} Projectors require their own power supply and optics calibration, which makes them unsuitable for portability on battery power.
    \item \textbf{Fixed Layouts:} There is an anticipated fixed key layout on the projector optics, and therefore the user will not be able to make any adjustments regarding the size of keys and arrangement of buttons.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Shadow-Based Monocular RGB Keyboards}
\label{subsec:lit_shadow_systems}
So that active laser lights and IR sensors would not be used, shadow analysis was performed during previous research on monocular RGB virtual keyboards \cite{Thomas2013Camera, Posner2012Single, Yue2014Blind}.

First among them was the one developed by Thomas \cite{Thomas2013Camera}, which involved the use of single-camera virtual keyboard in which it could be able to detect the movement of the fingers of the user and calculate the distance of the finger tip and its shadow in terms of pixels. As the finger moved towards the desktop, the distance between the two also reduced. Posner et al. \cite{Posner2012Single} enhanced the process of shadow detection using adaptive thresholding to detect touch contact on paper-based interfaces.

While shadow analysis allows one to detect touch even without the help of depth sensing cameras, there are many disadvantages of such an approach \cite{Yue2014Blind}, including:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Need for Efficient Directional Lighting:} Shadow tracking needs an efficient source of light at a particular angle. Light sources like ceiling lights that spread light or fluorescent lights can result in blurred or no shadows at all.
    \item \textbf{Self Occlusion in Hand:} The hand's palm and fingers often block light rays from reaching the table, and hence the shadow cast by the typing fingers becomes invisible.
    \item \textbf{Interference of Background on Surface:} Color, Pattern, or Reflective Nature of the Background Surface Is Interference for the Computer Vision Program in Recognizing Shadows on Background Causing Errors in Key Strikes \cite{Yue2014Blind}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Hardware-Assisted and Depth Sensor Systems (RGB-D / ToF)}
\label{subsec:lit_depth_systems}
Due to the advent of RGB-D sensors like the Microsoft Kinect, Intel RealSense, and Time-of-Flight sensors, virtual touch interfaces have become possible by determining the 3D coordinates of objects \cite{Lee2019Virtual, Toshpulatov2024RealTime}.

Work performed by Lee and Kwon \cite{Lee2019Virtual} was done in relation to the virtual touch sensor, based on the Time-of-Flight depth camera. Touch recognition was performed based on point cloud detection of fingertips that were a few millimeters apart from the surface, by calibrating reference depth plane of the surface of the table. The touch contact detection system was enhanced by Toshpulatov et al. \cite{Toshpulatov2024RealTime} through the inclusion of 3D depth sensing and 2D optical flow vectors to eliminate any hovering and false touch contacts.

The depth camera faces no problem of shadowing that comes with the conventional RGB camera because it can measure the 3D distance. The problems faced by depth cameras are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Costly Equipment:} The depth camera equipment is quite costly compared to the webcam, and it is also not compatible with the inexpensive laptop/monitor.
    \item \textbf{Noise from Proximity Sensors and Minimum Distance:} The minimum distance for consumer depth sensors is between twenty and forty centimeters, and noise is produced during the measurement of depth when the surface is nearly flat table surfaces.
    \item \textbf{High Resource Needs for Processing:} Real-time 3D point cloud processing is more resource-intensive compared to other forms of data processing and demands the use of GPUs.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{3D Mid-Air Typing and AR/VR Floating Keyboards}
\label{subsec:lit_air_systems}
Inventions like Meta Quest and Apple Vision Pro have spurred scientists to come up with mid-air typing methods in which typing is done on a virtual keyboard through mid-air \cite{Boletsis2019TextInput, Enkhbat2020HandKey, Lee2022Virtual, Yoo2026WordLevel}.

HandKey method was suggested by Enkhbat et al. \cite{Enkhbat2020HandKey}. It is a dual-hand air typing system that makes use of one camera. HandKey can detect the movements of fingers in 3D space using Convolutional Neural Network, in which selected keys are detected as fingertips move near pre-defined 3D virtual cubes. Two-handed virtual keyboards for headsets have been developed by Lee et al. \cite{Lee2022Virtual} using deep learning keypoint detection. Learning word-level motion has been proposed by Yoo and Lee \cite{Yoo2026WordLevel} as a means of classification of mid-air typing.

Even though mid-air typing is done without using a desk and paper, the usability tests have revealed that there are a number of issues:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Gorilla Arm Syndrome:} Fatigue of Arms occurs when people hold up their arms in high positions for extended periods of time \cite{Boletsis2019TextInput}.
    \item \textbf{No Physical Barrier:} When we type on the surface of the paper or the table, the finger movement gets stopped at the very instant when it touches the surface. But when we type in the air, there is no such physical barrier, which enables fingers to move through the keys \cite{Boletsis2019TextInput, Maman2023TypeNet}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Paper-Anchored and Monocular RGB Keyboards}
\label{subsec:lit_paper_systems}
This means that it may be said that the topic of this research paper is focused on one particular area where paper layouts along with RGB cameras are used for typing \cite{Zhang2001Visual, Srivastava2012RealTime, Khare2019QWERTY, Maman2023TypeNet}.

At first, Zhang et al. \cite{Zhang2001Visual} proposed the Visual Panel approach where monocular vision is employed to detect the outline of the paper sheet, and then the $H$ matrix is computed to get the location of the fingertip on the paper. Srivastava and Tripathi \cite{Srivastava2012RealTime} developed the velocity computation technique of the typing finger, where the finger quickly moves downwards and suddenly decelerates when it comes in contact with the paper surface. The methodology followed by Khare \cite{Khare2019QWERTY} to detect keys involved skin color segmentation and key region masking method.

New innovations include TypeNet \cite{Maman2023TypeNet}, which is a deep learning model that can enable touch typing on planar surfaces without calibration using only one camera. TypeNet uses the self-refinement approach combined with language modeling decoding to translate finger joint movements into text characters.

Despite all these changes, some of the limitations of conventional paper keyboards are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Markerless Tracking:} Previous methods entailed manual detection of corners or thresholding based on color alone \cite{Zhang2001Visual, Khare2019QWERTY}. As soon as there is any movement of paper or alteration in light levels, everything becomes impossible.
    \item \textbf{Speed of Pixel Thresholds:} The algorithms used to determine speed employ fixed pixel speed thresholds \cite{Srivastava2012RealTime}. Nonetheless, as the speed at which the pixels move differs depending on the distance between the camera and the hands, this threshold is not suitable for all people.
    \item \textbf{Fixed Design Layout Systems:} TypeNet \cite{Maman2023TypeNet} and HandKey \cite{Enkhbat2020HandKey} employ a fixed design of the QWERTY layout; however, neither can accommodate designing customized layouts with corresponding actions sequences.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Video Keystroke Inference and Attack Side-Channels}
\label{subsec:lit_attack_systems}
Video-based keystroke inference is another interesting field of research in which experts examine the chances of keystroke reconstruction using videos \cite{Yue2014Blind, Yang2022Towards}.

Yue et al.'s \cite{Yue2014Blind} team proved that it is possible to obtain the text written by a person with the help of videos which capture the actions of a person while he types on the keyboard of his mobile phone from the side view position. The technique included the use of optical flow to detect finger slowdown, the use of homography matrix $H$ for correspondence of the edges of the phone with that of the keyboard templates, and the use of shadow information to ensure contact. The approach was extended to an uncalibrated camera setting by Yang et al. \cite{Yang2022Towards} where self-supervised movements and trajectories were employed for acquiring typed letters using raw video without calibrating the layouts.

While these experiments are primarily focused on the danger of eavesdropping, there is a lot of useful data regarding computer vision technology, like homography calculation, deceleration of motion, and coordinates mapping.

\subsection{Specialized Optical, Acoustic, and Sensor-Fusion Modalities}
\label{subsec:lit_specialized_systems}
Apart from ordinary cameras, other forms of sensors have been analyzed for use including the Visible Light Communication Shadow Sensor, fingernail blood volume deformation sensor, and the motion capture sensor for musical instruments \cite{Webber2021Finger, Sun2009Estimation, MacRitchie2015Integrating, Pilkov2024Markerless}.

Webber et al. \cite{Webber2021Finger} utilized indoor ceiling light sensor and photodiode array technologies in determining the finger movements by sensing the light change caused by finger movement. Sun et al. \cite{Sun2009Estimation} utilized a vision system in estimating the pressure exerted by the finger using Linear Discriminant Analysis technique through color change analysis of the fingernails, thus illustrating the effect of finger pressure on color change of fingernails due to blood redistribution beneath the fingernails. MacRitchie, McPherson, and Pilkov \cite{MacRitchie2015Integrating, Pilkov2024Markerless} have been successful in demonstrating the effect of slowing down the finger joints due to their impact on the surface by employing the technique of movement capture of multiple camera movements together with key sensors in the piano.

It can be seen that changes occur in the movements of a person upon touching an object, thus proving the theory of detection of touching of paper through movements of joints in hands.

\subsection{Gaze-Based and Assistive Visual Interfaces}
\label{subsec:lit_gaze_interfaces}
Another type of technology that can be used for people having difficulty in controlling their movements is the eye gaze typing device which enables users to enter text without touching anything with the help of an eye gaze virtual keyboard. The eye gaze typing technology was invented by Chen \cite{Chen2024Gaze} using normal webcams and eye tracking was done through eye positions.

Although this option may offer certain ease in terms of access, there are also issues associated with the use of gaze keyboards. One of the issues is the Midas Touch issue, which means that the user may accidentally click when pausing to read because of the issue, hence making the keyboard less efficient than multi-fingered typing by hands.

\subsection{Robotic Vision Servo Control and Fiducial Calibration Systems}
\label{subsec:lit_robotics_systems}
Visual fiducial markers are often employed in robotic applications for positioning and camera calibration \cite{Ivacko2024Edge, Herdiansyah2024Implementation, Schenck2024Utilizing, Isaksson2020Camera, Oscadal2020Improved, GarridoJurado2026ArUco}.

Transformation of coordinates in gestures of robot arm movements was done by means of wrist cameras and ArUco markers by Ivacko et al. \cite{Ivacko2024Edge}. The camera calibration technique by Zhang was used by Herdiansyah et al. \cite{Herdiansyah2024Implementation} to enhance the localization performance of the ArUco markers up to 95\%. Schenck et al. \cite{Schenck2024Utilizing} created a pipeline for editing images in which synthetic deletion of markers was used to train markerless keypoint detection models in robots. ArUco Nano is an ArUco marker detection model introduced by Garrido-Jurado et al. \cite{GarridoJurado2026ArUco} and runs 6.5 times faster due to contour scanning technique.

The following experiments with robots prove that fiducial markers can ensure the accurate and reliable planar alignment, which makes them very suitable for calibrating paper keyboards using homography.

\subsection{Comparative Analysis of Machine Learning Models in Virtual Input}
\label{subsec:lit_ml_comparative}
The earlier studies have made use of a number of different machine learning approaches for turning visual cues into keystrokes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Limitations of Heuristics and Rule-Based Approach:} Training phase is not necessary here, but this approach will not work in case of any change in the speed of typing and in lighting of the user \cite{Srivastava2012RealTime, Posner2012Single}.
    \item \textbf{Support Vector Machines and Random Forests:} Fast training on sets of features with spatial engineering but no memory of temporal sequence \cite{Webber2021Finger}.
    \item \textbf{One-Dimensional Convolutional Neural Networks:} Fast Motion Pattern Extraction Using Sliding Windows without Storing any Hidden Recurrent State \cite{Lai2018CNN, Ikram2020Skeleton}.
    \item \textbf{Long Short Term Memory (LSTM) Neural Networks:} Maintain memory cell state throughout the series of time steps, allowing for learning of approach, impact, and recoil behavior by the neural network \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM, Doan2022Efficient, GilMartin2023Hand}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Noise Modeling and Lighting Robustness in Passive Vision Systems}
\label{subsec:lit_noise_modeling}
Uncontrolled deployment of monocular RGB vision systems in the indoor environment requires that the following be considered:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Diverse Lighting Conditions:} There exist diverse lighting conditions starting from poor indoor lighting (100~lux) to good indoor lighting (1,000~lux), as well as outdoor lighting (50,000+~lux). However, the current skin segmentation and shadow detection approaches cannot accommodate diverse lighting conditions \cite{Yue2014Blind}.
    \item \textbf{Deep Keypoint Detection Models Resilience:} The deep learning based keypoint detectors like MediaPipe Hands \cite{Zhang2020MediaPipe} have been trained with data sets having images at various contrast levels, various brightness levels, and also with some noise in them. This is done to ensure that the joints can be detected at various lighting conditions inside the room.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Hand Pose Estimation and Skeletal Joint Tracking}
Locating the joints of the hands both in two dimensions and three dimensions through real time processing of video input without the use of any glove or marker is vital for vision based interaction \cite{Cui2020Deep, Zhang2020MediaPipe}.

Color space segmentation based on skin color in color spaces such as HSV and YCbCr was applied for hand tracking in previous research \cite{Ji2018Local, Khare2019QWERTY}. Segmentation using skin color is highly susceptible to changes in the lighting of the room, complexity of the background, and skin color variations. Deep learning models such as OpenPose, YOLO-Pose \cite{Andriyanov2025Improving}, and Google MediaPipe Hand Landmarker \cite{Zhang2020MediaPipe} enhanced hand tracking through skeletal points estimation.

The Google MediaPipe Hands approach \cite{Zhang2020MediaPipe} depends on a two-stage detection and tracking pipeline that is carefully tuned to run on CPUs:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Detector for Palm Detection in a Single Shot:} In this case, the detection algorithm searches for the palm and wrist box in the whole video frame. It should be noted that the detection algorithm will not detect the movement of the fingers since it can interfere and may happen at various angles. It uses focal loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{focal}} = -\alpha_t (1 - p_t)^\gamma \log(p_t)$ to find hands accurately across different sizes.
    \item \textbf{21 Landmark Keypoint Network:} After the palm area has been localized, the landmark network is responsible for estimating the 2D coordinates $(x_i, y_i)$ and depth $z_i$ for each of the 21 landmarks that exist in the hand, as illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:mediapipe_hand_topology}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Spatial Kinematic Scale Normalization vs. Temporal Smoothing}
Coordinates determined based on the use of the landmarks would vary according to whether or not there is a change in the distance between the hand and the camera \cite{Doan2022Efficient, Kumar2026Hybrid}. This is because the distance between the camera will affect the size of the pixels that would be created. When the raw pixel coordinates are used to make a tap on the screen, the tap done by the larger hand that is closer to the camera would be much bigger than that of the smaller hand.

\paragraph{Mathematical Proof of Perspective Scale Invariance:}
The projection of 3D coordinates into 2D pixel coordinates is accomplished by use of the standard pinhole camera projection model through the use of the focal length $f$ of the camera:
\begin{equation}
x_{\text{pixel}} = f \frac{X}{Z}, \quad y_{\text{pixel}} = f \frac{Y}{Z}
\label{eq:pinhole_projection}
\end{equation}

When the distance of the hand from the camera is scaled by $k$ ($Z' = k Z$), the coordinates will have a reciprocal relationship with $k$:
\begin{equation}
x'_{\text{pixel}} = \frac{x_{\text{pixel}}}{k}, \quad y'_{\text{pixel}} = \frac{y_{\text{pixel}}}{k}
\label{eq:pixel_scaling}
\end{equation}

In a similar manner, the length of the hand $L_{\text{hand}}$ in the image plane is also reduced by the scaling factor $k$:
\begin{equation}
L'_{\text{hand}} = \frac{L_{\text{hand}}}{k}
\label{eq:hand_length_scaling}
\end{equation}

Since the key positions have been standardized based on the length of the hand, the effect of scaling on the key positions relative to the wrist becomes nullified:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{P}_i^{\text{norm}\prime} = \frac{\mathbf{P}_i' - \mathbf{P}_0'}{L'_{\text{hand}}} = \frac{\frac{\mathbf{P}_i - \mathbf{P}_0}{k}}{\frac{L_{\text{hand}}}{k}} = \frac{\mathbf{P}_i - \mathbf{P}_0}{L_{\text{hand}}} = \mathbf{P}_i^{\text{norm}}
\label{eq:scale_invariance_proof}
\end{equation}

The proof provided by Equation~(\ref{eq:scale_invariance_proof}) demonstrates the way normalization of unitless hand length leads to obtaining feature invariance irrespective of the distance between the camera and the hand size.

The wrist point would be taken as $\mathbf{P}_0 = (x_0, y_0)$ (Landmark 0) while the point at which the middle metacarpophalangeal joint would be $\mathbf{P}_9 = (x_9, y_9)$ (Landmark 9). Hand length scale factor $L_{\text{hand}}$ is illustrated below:
\begin{equation}
L_{\text{hand}} = \sqrt{(x_9 - x_0)^2 + (y_9 - y_0)^2}
\label{eq:hand_length_scale_def}
\end{equation}

Coordinates for landmark points are standardized to wrist landmark reference $\mathbf{P}_0$ and scaled to hand length scale factor $L_{\text{hand}}$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{P}_i^{\text{norm}} = \left( \frac{x_i - x_0}{L_{\text{hand}}}, \frac{y_i - y_0}{L_{\text{hand}}} \right)
\label{eq:normalized_landmark_def}
\end{equation}

Numerical velocity between two consecutive frames is found from these normalized coordinates:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{V}_i(t) = \frac{\mathbf{P}_i^{\text{norm}}(t) - \mathbf{P}_i^{\text{norm}}(t - \Delta t)}{\Delta t}
\label{eq:joint_velocity_def}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Direct Raw Kinematic Feature Propagation and Information Value:}
In the context of the applications for contactless interaction, including air drawing and gestures in mid-air, the application of the low pass or moving average filter is employed to remove hand tremors \cite{Kumar2026Hybrid}.

But when typing is being done through the activity on the surface, there is a need that the coordinates and the velocities be directly sent to the model without doing any temporal filtering. The reasons for doing feature propagation directly in touch recognition include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Spatial Ambiguity Bottleneck (Hover vs.\ Contact):} A finger that hovers above the paper keys in air 2~mm above it would result in the same spatial coordinates when compared to one that touches the surface. Spatial coordinates alone don't reveal the difference between hovering and touching. Touch is an act of collision in which there occurs an intense deceleration while hitting the desktop ($V_y \to 0$) \cite{Srivastava2012RealTime, MacRitchie2015Integrating}.
    \item \textbf{Effects of Deceleration by Touch:} The effect of deceleration by touch is caused by typing. The deceleration effects are performed within the time period of less than 40~ms. The smoothing filter eliminates the large changes in the velocity.
    \item \textbf{Biomechanical Joint Positions for Intent Distinction:} Use of multi-level joint positions in biomechanics, which include positions for fingertip (contact point), knuckles (curvature point), and wrist base (reference point of the palm), enables distinction of individual finger action from hand posture variations in deep sequence model.
    \item \textbf{Latency Time Delay Elimination:} Time delay results from the low pass filter as well as moving average. When considering live typing situation, there will be more time delay because of the few frames (30 to 90~ms).
\end{itemize}

With the hand length normalization ($\mathbf{P}_i^{\text{norm}}$) and the direct transfer of features, it becomes possible to preserve the sharp velocity changes, which allow the RNN to detect surface contacts efficiently.

\subsubsection{Planar Homography Mathematics and AprilTag Fiducial Tracking}
The transformation from the pixels $(x_{\text{pixel}}, y_{\text{pixel}})$ captured by the 2D camera to the piece of paper $(x_{\text{XML}}, y_{\text{XML}})$ involves calibrating the images that are tilted and distorted by the camera \cite{LonguetHiggins1985Machine, Pirchheim2011Homography, Wang2020Relative}.

The projection from the paper plane to the coordinate plane, since both of them are 2D planes, is carried out using a $3 \times 3$ homography matrix $H \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ of projective geometry \cite{LonguetHiggins1985Machine, Pirchheim2011Homography}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{bmatrix}
x_{\text{XML}} w \\
y_{\text{XML}} w \\
w
\end{bmatrix}
=
H \mathbf{P}_{\text{pixel}}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
h_{11} & h_{12} & h_{13} \\
h_{21} & h_{22} & h_{23} \\
h_{31} & h_{32} & h_{33}
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
x_{\text{pixel}} \\
y_{\text{pixel}} \\
1
\end{bmatrix}
\label{eq:homography_equation_def}
\end{equation}

The XML coordinates can be acquired after division with the scale factor $w = h_{31} x_{\text{pixel}} + h_{32} y_{\text{pixel}} + h_{33}$:
\begin{equation}
x_{\text{XML}} = \frac{h_{11} x_{\text{pixel}} + h_{12} y_{\text{pixel}} + h_{13}}{h_{31} x_{\text{pixel}} + h_{32} y_{\text{pixel}} + h_{33}}, \quad
y_{\text{XML}} = \frac{h_{21} x_{\text{pixel}} + h_{22} y_{\text{pixel}} + h_{23}}{h_{31} x_{\text{pixel}} + h_{32} y_{\text{pixel}} + h_{33}}
\label{eq:homography_cartesian_def}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Direct Linear Transform (DLT) Matrix Formulation:}
Equations generated using four or more pairs of corresponding points $(x_k, y_k) \leftrightarrow (x'_k, y'_k)$ ($N \ge 4$) will be:
\begin{align}
-x_k h_{11} - y_k h_{12} - h_{13} + 0 + 0 + 0 + x_k x'_k h_{31} + y_k x'_k h_{32} + x'_k h_{33} &= 0 \label{eq:dlt_row1} \\
0 + 0 + 0 - x_k h_{21} - y_k h_{22} - h_{23} + x_k y'_k h_{31} + y_k y'_k h_{32} + y'_k h_{33} &= 0 \label{eq:dlt_row2}
\end{align}

When these two equations are combined, we get the system matrix equation $\mathbf{A}_{2N \times 9} \mathbf{h} = \mathbf{0}$. In order to solve the system using the SVD technique ($\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{U} \mathbf{\Sigma} \mathbf{V}^T$), we obtain the homography vector $\mathbf{h}$ from the column in $\mathbf{V}$ that has the smallest singular value.

\paragraph{Comparative Analysis of Visual Fiducial Marker Systems:}
The fiducials must be placed at the corners of the printed image in order for them to act as references during homography \cite{Fiala2010Designing, Wang2016AprilTag2, Kallwies2020Determining, Shabalina2018ARTag, Kalaitzakis2021Fiducial, GarridoJurado2026ArUco, Zhang2022DeepTag}. There have been three main stages in the evolution of fiducial marker tracking on a plane surface:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Foundational Edge-Based Digital Tags (ARTag):} ARTag architecture concept was introduced by Fiala \cite{Fiala2010Designing} and it consisted of two phases; one was the candidate quads generation using edge gradient method and second was digital validation using forward error correction. It solved the problems of poor lighting condition and false positives in template-correlation based methods.
    \item \textbf{Line Clustering Based on Sub-Pixel Level and Hamming Code Methods (AprilTag and ArUco):} As far as the localization of corners is concerned, both AprilTag \cite{Wang2016AprilTag2} and ArUco \cite{GarridoJurado2026ArUco} used the similar approach where sub-pixel level gradient-based line clustering and thresholding algorithms were involved. According to comparative analyses conducted by Shabalina et al. \cite{Shabalina2018ARTag}, Kallwies et al. \cite{Kallwies2020Determining} and Kalaitzakis et al. \cite{Kalaitzakis2021Fiducial}, the smallest median corner error, which is equal to 0.017 pixels, belongs to AprilTag despite being tilted to $75^\circ$.
    \item \textbf{Deep Tagging Keypoints Detector (DeepTag):} DeepTag by Zhang et al. \cite{Zhang2022DeepTag} uses a fully convolutional neural network to detect digital keypoints markers under conditions of high motion blur and extremely oblique viewpoints.
\end{enumerate}

Although the marker detection approaches using deep learning techniques such as DeepTag \cite{Zhang2022DeepTag} have excellent robustness, the conventional AprilTag marker detection algorithm \cite{Wang2016AprilTag2} takes less than 1.0~millisecond per frame on the CPU in sub-pixel precision. This will provide us with the ability to do parallel processing of MediaPipe joints and PyTorch touch detection without the use of GPU in a regular CPU. Therefore, border AprilTags are preferred for our paper keyboard.

\subsubsection{Deep Sequential Temporal Touch Detection Models}
Detection of touches based on normalized landmarks of the finger surface takes place via the analysis of motion patterns in various frames \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM, Nunez2018Convolutional, Gammulle2021TMMF}.

The velocity based approach such as clicking when the speed of the falling object exceeds a particular value ($V_y > \theta$) is not very effective because of the hovering effect and finger coupling causing variations in the speed \cite{Simao2016Unsupervised, Toshpulatov2024RealTime}.

RNNs, particularly LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory) networks \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM}, can be used effectively in motion-based data classification \cite{Doan2022Efficient, GilMartin2023Hand, Nunez2018Convolutional, Ikram2020Skeleton}. The primary features that the LSTM network utilizes for pattern recognition from sequences are:
\begin{align}
\mathbf{f}_t &= \sigma(\mathbf{W}_f [\mathbf{h}_{t-1}, \mathbf{x}_t] + \mathbf{b}_f) \quad &&\text{(Forget Gate)} \label{eq:lstm_forget_def} \\
\mathbf{i}_t &= \sigma(\mathbf{W}_i [\mathbf{h}_{t-1}, \mathbf{x}_t] + \mathbf{b}_i) \quad &&\text{(Input Gate)} \label{eq:lstm_input_def} \\
\tilde{\mathbf{C}}_t &= \tanh(\mathbf{W}_c [\mathbf{h}_{t-1}, \mathbf{x}_t] + \mathbf{b}_c) \quad &&\text{(Candidate State)} \label{eq:lstm_candidate_def} \\
\mathbf{C}_t &= \mathbf{f}_t \odot \mathbf{C}_{t-1} + \mathbf{i}_t \odot \tilde{\mathbf{C}}_t \quad &&\text{(Cell State Update)} \label{eq:lstm_cell_def} \\
\mathbf{o}_t &= \sigma(\mathbf{W}_o [\mathbf{h}_{t-1}, \mathbf{x}_t] + \mathbf{b}_o) \quad &&\text{(Output Gate)} \label{eq:lstm_output_def} \\
\mathbf{h}_t &= \mathbf{o}_t \odot \tanh(\mathbf{C}_t) \quad &&\text{(Hidden Output)} \label{eq:lstm_hidden_def}
\end{align}

\paragraph{Comparative Deep Neural Architecture Evaluation:}
While designing one-dimensional time series models for hand skeleton, the following five deep learning models have been considered \cite{Lai2018CNN, Ikram2020Skeleton, Nunez2018Convolutional, Doan2022Efficient, Gammulle2021TMMF}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Unidirectional LSTM Networks:} Data processing takes place sequentially from the current frame to the previous frame ($\mathbf{X} = (\mathbf{x}_1, \dots, \mathbf{x}_T)$), making them suitable for real-time streaming since the future frames cannot be accessed yet.
    \item \textbf{Bi-directional Long Short Term Memory (BiLSTM):} It uses both forward ($\overrightarrow{\mathbf{h}}_t$) and backward ($\overleftarrow{\mathbf{h}}_t$) hidden layers. Even though BiLSTM gives very precise output when using pre-recorded data, in order to do the backward process, future frames are needed, which leads to buffer delay.
    \item \textbf{One-Dimensional Convolutional Neural Network (One-Dimensional CNN):} Using one-dimensional temporal filters on the features of the landmarks \cite{Lai2018CNN, Ikram2020Skeleton}. One-dimensional CNN can rapidly recognize local features, but it cannot preserve memory states for larger movements.
    \item \textbf{One-Dimensional Residual Networks (1D ResNets):} Utilize skip connections ($\mathbf{y} = \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}) + \mathbf{x}$) to aid in training deep temporal convolutional neural networks without vanishing gradients.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Head Attention (Attention):} Employ multi-head attention ($\text{Attention}(\mathbf{Q}, \mathbf{K}, \mathbf{V}) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q} \mathbf{K}^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) \mathbf{V}$) in order to make dependencies among all temporal windows of the sequence.
\end{enumerate}

Our uni-directional LSTM PyTorch model for training five frames of scale-normalized input data turned out to be the perfect choice for this experiment thanks to its high accuracy and minimal CPU latency (\texttt{run\_all.py}).

\paragraph{Temporal Windowing Architecture:}
The sliding windows technique strikes an optimal balance between computation efficiency and accuracy due to the processing of information received on landmarks by partitioning the input features in time windows \cite{Simao2016Unsupervised, Doan2022Efficient, Gammulle2021TMMF}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{5 Frames Window with 2 Frames Overlapping (Stride of 3 Frames):} For example, for 5 frames, the total time period is 416.6~ms ($T = 416.6$~ms) for 12~FPS video. But when there are 2 frames with stride of 3 frames, it implies that PyTorch LSTM processes after touching every 250~ms. From experimental observations, it was concluded that 5 frames provided sufficient time period for the process of finger's descent, touch on surface, and rebound while keeping latency low.
\end{itemize}

As per the performance standards of different two-dimensional architecture systems, the implementation of multi-layer PyTorch LSTM network model with normalized five frame sequence ensures efficient multi-finger touch detection on ordinary computer devices.

\subsubsection{Layout Customization and Synchronized XML Artifacts}
The major limitation identified by previous researchers is that there is no coupling between the layout editor and the runtime typing engine \cite{ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020, Maman2023TypeNet}. All the keyboards designed in previous literature have the keyboard layouts of the QWERTY keyboard built into the software.

It is done by designing an architecture where another piece of software named Layout Designer (\texttt{designer/designer\_app.py}) will be used, where you can design the shape of the button, assign keys to specific keystrokes or commands, and place AprilTags on the corners of the layout. Once the layout is designed, the software will generate two synchronized files:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Layout for Printable Output (PDF):} The document has everything that is needed regarding the borders and AprilTags that must be printed on regular paper.
    \item \textbf{Layout XML Document Definition:} An XML document that has details about the coordinates of keys $(x_{\min}, y_{\min}, x_{\max}, y_{\max})$, label, action command, and anchors of markers.
\end{enumerate}

During operation, the application program will utilize the XML file to determine the lookup boundaries and the homography targets without having to recompile the source code.

\section{Reflection and Synthesis of Research Gap}
\label{sec:lit_reflection}
Through a detailed review of the literature, it is possible to see what has been achieved, but also some limitations that have not yet been solved by this research area.

\subsection{Synthesis of Prior Literature Limitations}
After analyzing the existing systems, it can be concluded that there are six main constraints in previous researches:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Inability to Track Multiple Fingers Independently:} The traditional method using monocular camera based system would be able to track just one finger at a particular instance of time, and that would either be the index finger or the fastest finger \cite{Thomas2013Camera, Ji2018Local, Srivastava2012RealTime}. When we consider the shadow based technique, shadows formed by different fingers in synchronization would overlap and create interference among themselves \cite{Posner2012Single}. Hence, the earlier methods are not capable of identifying natural touches of all the five fingers of the hand.
    \item \textbf{Reliance on Artificial Dwell-Time Delays:} Because 2D cameras cannot detect the physical touch, traditional vision keyboards have the problem that the user had to keep his/her finger on top of the key for some time period called dwell time (500~ms to 1000~ms) \cite{Khare2019QWERTY, Chen2024Gaze}. This unnecessary delay is one of the major factors leading to the input lag and user fatigue.
    \item \textbf{High Computing Overhead and GPU Requirements on Commodity Hardware:} However, the most sophisticated algorithms used in vision-based deep learning applications, using high-resolution imagery and 3D grids, can only reach a maximum speed of 15 frames per second even on a common computer \cite{Enkhbat2020HandKey, Ji2018Local}, while some others rely on GPU workstations to run in real-time.
    \item \textbf{Environmental Brittleness Under Varying Lighting and Shadows:} Nevertheless, the shadow detection techniques and color thresholding methods fall short in scenarios involving diffuse lighting conditions, multiple lights, soft hand shadows, and varied skin and background colors \cite{Thomas2013Camera, Yue2014Blind, ReviewVirtualKeyboard2020}.
    \item \textbf{Reliance on Expensive or Specialized Hardware:} Most current approaches that try to solve the problem of ambiguity in determining the depth from a monocular image require the use of specialized equipment like ToF camera, stereo vision camera, lasers, and even special gloves with sensors \cite{Lee2019Virtual, Kudale2016RealTime, Toshpulatov2024RealTime, Pilkov2024Markerless}. As a result, this makes the approaches expensive and not suitable for running on standard computers with only a regular webcam.
    \item \textbf{Lack of Integrated Layout Customization, Marker Tracking, and Action Multiplexing:} The recent typing system based on deep learning like TypeNet \cite{Maman2023TypeNet} and HandKey \cite{Enkhbat2020HandKey} use the fixed QWERTY keyboard and do not have any means for printing out the sheets with the tracking markers. Moreover, due to the absence of homography tracking mechanism ($H$) in these typing systems, even a small shift in sheets results in the inaccuracy of coordinates \cite{Wang2016AprilTag2, Kallwies2020Determining, Pirchheim2011Homography}. In addition, there is no dynamic multiplexing facility in these typing systems.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Formal Rationale and Research Gap Justification}
These limitations form the research gap on which the thesis study is founded:

\begin{quote}
\emph{The fact that it does not have a virtual keyboard layout system which is coherent and functions real-time using only one monocular RGB camera setup, which uses only the printed papers layout with no hardware attached to it, recognizes the presence of multiple fingers independently without any artificial delay, works real-time only using CPUs without using any GPUs, can carry out dynamic multiplexing actions in only one printed paper layout, and works well in normal and low camera resolution at the standard of 12~FPS.}
\end{quote}

In fact, the custom paper layout design, AprilTag homography detection, hand-length normalization on a unit basis ($L_{\text{hand}}$), feature propagation, and LSTM touch classifier on PyTorch framework have addressed all the limitations of the previous methods, which comprised lighting problems, high cost, single finger touch, delay in dwell time, high computational cost, and fixed paper layout designs.
